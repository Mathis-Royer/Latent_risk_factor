## Five questions

**1. Purpose and original intent.** Turn the encoder's fixed, shape-only exposure matrix into a complete `n×n` asset covariance, so that the portfolio optimiser sees all inter-stock dependence through a low-dimensional, hopefully better-conditioned channel: `Σ_assets = B̃^port Σ_z B̃^portᵀ + D_ε` (`DVT_strategie_facteurs_risque_latents_v4_1.md:710`, `:757`). The stated payoff is comparative: the factor route "enables a **factor-based covariance estimation** that is far more stable than direct estimation in asset space" (`DVT_strategie_facteurs_risque_latents_v4_1.md:13`), with the closure assertion "All inter-stock correlation passes through the factors" (`:761`). The design's distinguishing device is a **dual rescaling** — date-specific vol for recovering historical factor returns, current-date vol for portfolio construction (`:724`, `:728`, `:742`; `ISD_vae_latent_risk_factors.md:1692–1706`, INV-004 at `:1792`). Everything downstream (λ_k, entropy weights, min-variance) consumes this object.

**2. State of the art for this objective.** (i) Approximate-factor-model covariance with *estimated* factors: Fan, Fan & Lv (2008, JASA) show factor-based covariance is consistent and dominates the sample covariance only when k diverges at a controlled rate *and* the residual covariance is sparse — this is the precise theorem the unit needs and never invokes [unverified]. (ii) Sparse residuals rather than a diagonal: Fan, Liao & Mincheva (2013, JRSS-B, POET) threshold the residual covariance instead of discarding it [unverified]. (iii) Shrinkage: Ledoit & Wolf (2004, JMVA) requires i.i.d. sampling; Ledoit & Wolf (2017, RFS, "Markowitz meets Goldilocks") shows *nonlinear* shrinkage dominates linear shrinkage for portfolio selection [unverified]. (iv) HAC inference for overlapping/serial dependence: Newey & West (1987, Econometrica) [unverified]. (v) The error-maximisation objection to plug-in covariance in min-variance: Michaud (1989, FAJ) [unverified]; Jagannathan & Ma (2003, JF) [unverified]. (vi) The deep-learning formulation that would actually add value here: Chen, Pelger & Zhu (2024, Management Science, "Deep learning in asset pricing") build a **conditional** factor model whose loadings are functions of characteristics and whose factors are pinned by adversarial GMM moment conditions — i.e. the network is trained so that its factors *are* the best cross-sectional regressors, not merely good autoencoders [unverified]. Gu, Kelly & Xiu (2020, RFS) for the broader NN asset-pricing evidence [unverified].

**3. What prevents it from reaching that.** Findings CRI-01 to CRI-05 and MAJ-06 below: the covariance dimension is set by an arithmetic error (CRI-01), the small-sample guard assumes i.i.d. that the construction destroys (CRI-02), the shrinkage moves the primary objective and is not matched in the benchmark arm (CRI-03), the residual structure is asserted diagonal and is biased in a specific direction (CRI-04), the diagnostic that is supposed to certify the model cannot do so (CRI-05), and nothing in the pipeline makes the exposure matrix a good cross-sectional regressor (MAJ-06).

**4. Real vs self-imposed constraints.** *Real*: n ≫ T; a point-in-time universe with delistings (`DVT_strategie_facteurs_risque_latents_v4_1.md:281`, `:736`); genuine non-stationarity of factor correlations (conceded at `:1123`). *Self-imposed*: equal weighting of 30 years as an axiom (`sigma_z_ewma_justification.md:5`, `:111–115`); diagonal `D_ε` (`DVT…:761`); isotropy as the shrinkage target (`:752`); a 252-day trailing vol as the deflator (`:734`); two rescaling regimes rather than a single conditional covariance; no HAC anywhere; the cap `AU_max_stat` computed with the wrong quadratic (CRI-01).

**5. What should be removed, merged or moved.** *Remove*: the separate "portfolio rescaling" as an independent object — merge both regimes into one explicit conditional covariance so the estimation/construction split stops hiding a fixed-vs-dynamic covariance discontinuity (MAJ-08). *Remove*: isotropy as the shrinkage target; it is a tuning parameter of the primary objective (CRI-03). *Remove*: `D_ε` diagonal as an axiom; replace with an estimated low-rank-plus-diagonal residual structure, which is also the natural place for the deep-learning value-add (CRI-04, alternative RC1-c). *Move*: the `AU` truncation decision out of the encoder (marginal KL, `:714`, `:718`) into the risk model (eigenvalue or risk contribution), since KL is the encoder's own training signal and not a risk quantity. *Move*: EP from an in-sample ratio to OOS covariance loss (CRI-05). *Merge*: `sigma_z_ewma_justification.md` should cease to exist as a "justification" and become one falsifiable premise with a stopping criterion (MAJ-10).

## Root-cause hypotheses

**RC1 — Inherited textbook statistics bolted onto an explicitly non-stationary premise.** The unit imports i.i.d. Ledoit-Wolf, a static factor covariance, diagonal residuals, and a per-date OLS — the standard approximate-factor-model kit — while the surrounding theory demands permanent crisis memory. These commitments are mutually incompatible, and the unit's response was to add rescaling and shrinkage rather than to state and test a *dynamic* covariance model. Symptoms: CRI-02, CRI-04, MAJ-08, MAJ-09, MIN-11.
- *Alternative A*: state a **conditional** `Σ_z,t` driven by a small learned state (dispersion, vol regime, VIX band), trained on an OOS covariance loss. Trade-off: reintroduces the reactive behaviour the philosophy rejects — but makes the reactive/anti-cyclical trade-off a *measured* quantity (a learned memory half-life) instead of an axiom, and it is falsifiable.
- *Alternative B*: a **two-component** `Σ_z` = permanent + transient with a learned decay, fitted by maximum likelihood or a deep state-space model. Trade-off: more parameters; preserves "crisis memory" as a claim that can fail (does the transient weight's estimate sit at 0?) rather than as an assertion.
- *Alternative C*: keep full-history `Σ_z` but **model the residual covariance** with a second, low-rank learned factor layer (K₂ ≪ n) plus diagonal. Trade-off: identification/rotation issues and a risk of fitting noise; requires a stopping criterion on the residual factor count — but it converts "all correlation passes through the factors" from an axiom into a measured approximation error.

**RC2 — The exposure matrix is trained for one objective and consumed by another.** `B̄` is optimised for window reconstruction and KL; `ẑ_t` treats it as a regression design matrix. No loss term, no diagnostic, and no stopping criterion connects the two. This single mismatch generates the unit's central unproven claim and its certifying metric. Symptoms: MAJ-06, CRI-05, MAJ-07.
- *Alternative A*: **adversarial/conditional** factor model — loadings conditioned on the stock's own history and factors pinned by moment conditions so that residuals are orthogonal to factors by construction (Chen–Pelger–Zhu style [unverified]). Trade-off: the model becomes partly a return model, blurring the "pure risk" positioning; needs a moment-condition test to stay honest.
- *Alternative B*: multi-task objective — the same latent must reconstruct *and* minimise cross-sectional residual variance, with an explicit, reported weighting and a tested sensitivity to it. Trade-off: two objectives can conflict, generalising the very problem U08 already has with the co-movement scaffolding.
- *Alternative C*: keep `B̄` purely descriptive for interpretation, but let a **separate** learned map from windows to exposures be fitted on the risk objective. Trade-off: modularity and a clean division of labour versus the loss of the "one network, one object" story.

**RC3 — Regularisation is treated as hygiene rather than as a parameter of the decision.** δ, α, and `AU` all shift the same risk-vs-entropy trade-off, yet δ is chosen by a separate statistical rule, α by an "elbow" that moves with δ, and `AU` by the encoder's own KL ranking. Symptoms: CRI-01, CRI-03, MAJ-10.
- *Alternative A*: calibrate (δ, α) **jointly** on an OOS criterion, forbidding independent calibration. Trade-off: heavier computation, but removes the confound.
- *Alternative B*: replace linear shrinkage with a **learned eigenvalue transform** (a small map from the empirical spectrum to a shrunk spectrum, trained on a leave-one-out covariance loss). This is a legitimate deep-learning value-add: only AU parameters, low-dimensional, and it can learn to shrink the dormant-factor eigenvalues while leaving the active ones — precisely the non-stationarity the theory needs. Trade-off: risk of overfitting the spectrum; needs AU ≫ parameters, which CRI-02 says is not currently true.
- *Alternative C*: **drop shrinkage entirely** and use aggressive dimension control as the sole regulariser. Trade-off: loses the small-sample correction; but it removes the entropy confounding at a stroke and makes the comparison with the PCA arm symmetric.

**RC4 — Diagnostics are in-sample and scale-inconsistent.** EP, the AU/KL guard, and the invariance tests all certify properties of the *estimation* sample or of the code path, never the out-of-sample behaviour of the assembled `Σ_assets`. Symptoms: CRI-05, MAJ-06 (testing), MIN-11.
- *Alternative A*: replace EP with a proper OOS covariance battery — Stein loss, Frobenius loss on the correlation matrix, and the realised/predicted variance ratio — with shrinkage and rescaling **frozen at forecast date**. Trade-off: requires a genuine forecast/evaluate split inside each fold; kills several currently-quotable diagnostics.
- *Alternative B*: keep EP but make it scale-invariant (correlation-based, numerator and denominator from one rescaling) and always report in-sample alongside OOS. Trade-off: cheap, but only repairs the ratio, not the in-sample leverage floor.
- *Alternative C*: add a **residual-diagnostic gate** — cross-sectional mean |correlation| of residuals, eigenvalue spectrum of the residual correlation matrix, and a test that `Σ_assets` explains OOS portfolio variance better than a naive diagonal — as a hard precondition for the factor structure to be accepted. Trade-off: adds a layer of decisions and thresholds, itself a parameter-selection problem; but it is the only route by which CRI-04 can ever fail.

## Verdict

This unit is where the theory should be earning its deep-learning claim, and instead it does exactly the opposite: it takes a latent exposure matrix produced by an objective that never asked for explanatory power, then pushes it through the most classical linear machinery available — per-date OLS, Ledoit-Wolf shrinkage toward isotropy, diagonal idiosyncratic variances — and certifies the result with an in-sample ratio that mixes two rescalings. The unit's own justification document for the covariance window is a one-sided bias-variance argument that misapplies its source and touts "coherence" between a covariance weighting and a dimension cap as a virtue when it is a confound that makes the estimator experimentally unidentifiable. The small-sample guard is arithmetically wrong in the DVT and duplicated in a different form in the ISD, and the true effective sample size of the factor returns is one to two orders of magnitude below the number on which the guard is built. Five critical defects, all of which are internal to the formalisation: none requires data, code, or a benchmark result to establish. The steelman — that deflating factor returns by cross-sectional median volatility is a legitimate way to build a *scale-free* factor return series, that a factor model genuinely does concentrate the estimation problem in an `AU×AU` object, and that a diagonal residual is a defensible first-order simplification — is real but does not survive contact with the documents' own numbers and their own concessions. The structural repair is not more patches: it is to state a *dynamic* factor covariance (with the memory half-life as a learned, testable object rather than an axiom), to give the latent code an explicit cross-sectional pricing objective so that "far more stable than direct estimation" becomes a claim with a loss function behind it, to let the residual covariance be a modelled object rather than a diagonal, and to move every certification of the risk model out of sample and into a single, scale-consistent loss.

Score: 3/10
