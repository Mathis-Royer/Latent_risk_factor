## Five questions

1. **Purpose and original intent.** The unit is the portfolio-construction layer: it turns the VAE covariance into weights. The declared objective is "minimize risk through optimal diversification" (`DVT_strategie_facteurs_risque_latents_v4_1.md:25`), operationalized as a "two-level hierarchy: risk minimization is the primary goal, factorial diversification is the means" (`:765`). Default mode is μ=0 (`:776`), exploratory mode is a diagnostic (`:784`), directional mode is deferred (`:782`, `:1129-1137`). The ISD restates the same chain: compute H and ∇H, solve by SCA, enforce cardinality, calibrate α (`ISD_vae_latent_risk_factors.md:1821-1823`). The intent is a risk-only, long-only, low-turnover portfolio diversified across discovered factors—not a return-seeking optimizer.

2. **What a state-of-the-art unit would look like.** An explicit risk budget or risk target solved as a risk-budgeting problem, with convex or certified global optimization (Roncalli 2013, *Introduction to Risk Parity and Budgeting*; Maillard, Roncalli & Teïletche 2010, "The Properties of Equally Weighted Risk Contribution Portfolios", *JPM*; Spinu 2013, convex log-barrier ERC, quoted at `implementation_decisions.md:249-259`) [unverified except Spinu as quoted]. It would include the idiosyncratic bucket in the risk decomposition—the corpus itself proposes this (`literature_review_idiosyncratic_risk.md:236`)—use a stable factor basis, and treat estimation error explicitly: shrinkage (`DVT_strategie_facteurs_risque_latents_v4_1.md:752`), constraints-as-regularization (Jagannathan & Ma 2003, "Risk Reduction in Large Portfolios", *JF*) [unverified], and the 1/N evidence (DeMiguel, Garlappi & Uppal 2009, "Optimal Versus Naive Diversification", *RFS*, quoted at `literature_review_strategy.md:105-107`). Turnover would be priced in return units with a cost model (Almgren & Chriss 2000, "Optimal Execution of Portfolio Transactions", *Journal of Risk*; Gârleanu & Pedersen 2013, "Dynamic Trading with Predictable Returns and Transaction Costs", *JF*) [unverified]. Cardinality would use an MIQP/relaxation with a bound (Bertsimas & Cory-Wright 2022, "A Scalable Algorithm for Sparse Portfolio Selection") [unverified]. The operating point would be pre-registered or eliminated by a risk budget—not selected from an in-sample elbow.

3. **What prevents the current unit from reaching it.** The objective is not identified (CRI-01, CRI-06); the default mode is not neutral and is weakly dominated by its own α=0 case (CRI-02); the diversification functional is not a function of the risk model alone (CRI-03, CRI-04); the calibration and selection machinery is arbitrary and internally contradictory (CRI-05, CRI-07, CRI-08, CRI-09). The corpus's supporting documents already contain the missing pieces—the idiosyncratic pseudo-factor (`literature_review_idiosyncratic_risk.md:236`), the D6 warning about low-eigenvalue factors (`strategy_comparison.md:61`), and the DeMiguel evidence (`literature_review_strategy.md:104-107`)—but the main spec does not integrate them.

4. **Real constraints vs self-imposed choices.** Real: long-only, fully invested, position cap (`DVT_strategie_facteurs_risque_latents_v4_1.md:812`); non-concavity of risk-contribution entropy (`:818`); NP-hardness of the semi-continuous support (`:834`); estimation error in Σ_z (`:752`); no-CRSP data (briefing). Self-imposed: μ=0 as "neutral"; factor entropy as the diversification functional; SCA with 5 starts; the λ market-risk-aversion calibration; the α elbow/Kneedle; φ=25, κ₁=0.1, κ₂=7.5, δ̄=1%; w_min=0.1% at n=1000; the 30% cap; "monthly to quarterly"; treating the exploratory mode as a mode.

5. **What should be removed, merged or moved.** Remove: the μ=0/directional "modularity" framing (it is one objective with μ as an input); the λ market-risk-aversion calibration; the α elbow/Kneedle; the "Almgren-Chriss" label on the turnover penalty; the exploratory mode. Merge: factor entropy with an idiosyncratic pseudo-factor into a total-risk budget; the concentration penalty with the hard position cap into one explicit constraint; the turnover penalty with a turnover budget. Move: the diversification target out of the optimizer into the risk-model layer (define target risk contributions); the α/operating-point selection into the pre-registered OOS protocol; the rebalancing calendar into a fixed, benchmark-shared protocol.

## Root-cause hypotheses

**RC1 — Heterogeneous units plus free scalars (explains CRI-01, 05, 06, 09).** The optimizer is assembled by adding a variance term, a nats term, weight² terms and weight terms, each with its own scalar; no unit normalization, identification analysis or economic calibration is performed. Structural alternatives: (a) a risk-target formulation—maximize H subject to w'Σw ≤ σ_target² (or minimize variance subject to H ≥ H_min); trade-off: one parameter fewer, but σ_target must be chosen and the constraint may not bind; (b) return-equivalent costs—price φ and κ through a cost model and a risk-aversion parameter; trade-off: requires AUM/ADV calibration, but the scalars become interpretable; (c) hard constraints instead of penalties—replace P_conc with w≤3% and P_turn with a turnover budget; trade-off: nonsmooth, but no arbitrary scalars.

**RC2 — Entropy of squared factor exposures treated as a canonical risk functional (explains CRI-03, 04, 07).** The theory conflates "number of independent risk sources" with "entropy of estimated squared exposures" and treats the eigenbasis as canonical. Alternatives: (a) risk budgeting over total risk including an idiosyncratic pseudo-factor, with explicit budgets b_k (the corpus's own `literature_review_idiosyncratic_risk.md:236`); trade-off: budgets must be chosen, but the objective is convex and the solution is a real risk allocation; (b) keep entropy but on a stable basis—treat near-degenerate eigenvalues as one bucket and use H_total; trade-off: less granular, but well-defined and idio-aware; (c) replace entropy with a diversification ratio or Rao quadratic entropy (`literature_review_strategy.md:119-130`); trade-off: asset-level DR cannot detect latent concentration (the DVT's own objection), but it is scale-invariant and economically interpretable.

**RC3 — Risk-only identity asserted rather than modeled (explains CRI-02, 08, 10).** The product is defined by what it does not forecast, not by a risk mandate, so the implicit low-vol/beta bet and the absence of a risk target go unexamined. Alternatives: (a) an explicit risk mandate—target vol/beta and constrain the portfolio; trade-off: may require leverage/cash, incompatible with long-only fully-invested; (b) a beta-neutral or benchmark-relative formulation; trade-off: gives up the "absolute risk" claim but makes the implicit bet explicit; (c) keep μ=0 but report factor attribution and treat the low-vol tilt as a stated thesis; trade-off: honest, but no longer a pure risk strategy.

**RC4 — Calibration by geometric heuristics rather than decision theory (explains CRI-05, 06, 08).** The theory avoids choosing an economic trade-off and delegates the choice to a geometric feature of a 7-point in-sample curve. Alternatives: (a) nested walk-forward selection of α on a pre-registered OOS criterion (e.g., max Sharpe subject to a vol target, or min max drawdown); trade-off: more compute/overfitting risk, but falsifiable; (b) eliminate α via a risk budget; trade-off: no free diversification aggressiveness, but no calibration; (c) pre-register α from a utility argument (e.g., the implied risk-premium sacrifice); trade-off: may be wrong, but not data-snooped.

**RC5 — Combinatorial and non-convex parts patched post hoc (explains CRI-07, 11).** SCA, 5 starts and greedy elimination are layered onto a problem whose continuous part is convex and whose combinatorial part is NP-hard, without separating them. Alternatives: (a) separate support selection (MIQP/SOCP or a relaxation with a bound) from continuous risk budgeting (convex); trade-off: compute, but a bound; (b) convexify the whole problem as risk budgeting and drop the entropy; trade-off: no entropy, but globally solvable; (c) if entropy is retained, use a global method over the low-dimensional factor space (the two-stage route) with a certificate; trade-off: only feasible for small AU, but removes the 5-start heuristic.

## Verdict

The unit is where the risk model becomes a portfolio, and the only place where the theory must commit to a risk/return trade-off. It does not.
