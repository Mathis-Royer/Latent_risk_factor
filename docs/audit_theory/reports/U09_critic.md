## Five questions

**1. Purpose and original intent.** §4.5 exists to produce one encoder per fold whose latent geometry *is* the risk model's exposure map. Its three explicit jobs: (i) learn "a broad vocabulary of risk patterns across **all** historical regimes, not weighted toward the present", with relevance deferred to inference (`DVT_strategie_facteurs_risque_latents_v4_1.md:594-596`); (ii) provide the convergence criterion — "Early stopping is the actual convergence criterion" (`DVT_strategie_facteurs_risque_latents_v4_1.md:616`); (iii) supply a replayable deployment run at fixed $E^*$ so OOS metrics represent production (`DVT_strategie_facteurs_risque_latents_v4_1.md:898-900`). Everything else (Adam, LR, batch size) is instrumentation for those three.

**2. State of the art for this objective.** A SOTA version would not select a checkpoint on a likelihood it never validates against its own downstream object. It would: (a) rank checkpoints/covariance forecasts with a proxy-robust scoring rule — Patton (2011), *Volatility forecast comparison using imperfect volatility proxies*, J. Econometrics: MSE and QLIKE belong to a class robust to an imperfect conditionally-unbiased proxy for ranking forecasts `[unverified]`; (b) size the validation horizon so it contains several independent T-length windows and multiple regimes, embedding it in rolling-origin evaluation — Tashman (2000), *Out-of-sample tests of forecasting accuracy*, IJF `[unverified]`; (c) handle non-stationarity with a mechanism (time-varying latent dynamics, e.g. Krishnan, Shalit & Sontag (2017), *Structured Inference Networks for Nonlinear State Space Models*, AAAI `[unverified]`, or continual training with a stability penalty, Kirkpatrick et al. (2017), *Overcoming catastrophic forgetting in neural networks*, PNAS `[unverified]`) rather than with a uniform pool; (d) report seed dispersion and require the winning margin to exceed it — Bouthillier et al. (2021), *Accounting for Variance in Machine Learning Benchmarks*, MLSys `[unverified]`; (e) define the curriculum against a measured saturation signal, not a fraction of an epoch budget whose length is itself determined by the curriculum. Concretely, the deep-learning value-add that PCA cannot express is a *state-conditional exposure map* (loadings as a learned function of recent market state) plus a *learned aggregation* over the window trajectory; both are testable, both are currently absent (aggregation is deferred to Iteration 7, `DVT_strategie_facteurs_risque_latents_v4_1.md:1169-1177`).

**3. What prevents it.** C1 (validation set is one window deep), C2 (the curriculum clock is set by the stopping rule it should precede), C4 (the checkpoint criterion discards the crisis reweighting), C6 (no replication policy). C1 and C2 are independent; either alone makes the unit's convergence story unsupported.

**4. Real constraints vs self-imposed choices.** Real: expanding window on a record containing only a handful of crises; 6-month OOS × ~34 folds; 21-day embargo; no CRSP; a ~1,100-run compute envelope (`implementation_decisions.md:186`). Self-imposed: validation fixed at exactly 2 years — 5-10 years would still be point-in-time and is the single cheapest fix; max 100 epochs, patience 10/5, batch 256, one seed; "no validation set" in Phase B (a rolling validation block is available in production too, `DVT_strategie_facteurs_risque_latents_v4_1.md:898`); curriculum expressed in epoch fractions; full retraining from scratch; Mode P exclusivity.

**5. What should be removed, merged, moved.** *Remove*: the "uniform across all history" claim (it is false, C5); the epoch-fraction curriculum; the $E^*$ transfer; the 2-year validation block as an estimate of generalisation. *Merge*: early stopping, LR scheduling and checkpoint selection into one selection rule with one criterion; γ and checkpoint selection into one coherent weighted objective, or drop γ from training and put crisis emphasis where it survives — the aggregation weights. *Move*: model selection from the generative ELBO to the risk layer ($\Sigma_z$ forecast loss, QLIKE-style); retraining-cadence and fine-tuning decisions out of the training appendix into the walk-forward so they can be tested; crisis emphasis from the gradient into the composite profile, where it currently has no representation at all.

## Root-cause hypotheses

**R1 — Proxy objective, never checked against the object it serves.** The unit optimises and selects on window reconstruction, while the deliverable is $\Sigma_z$ and a portfolio entropy. Nothing in the unit measures whether a lower ELBO produces a better risk model; the corpus itself concedes a stable latent geometry can be "not aligned with actual risk dependencies" (`DVT_strategie_facteurs_risque_latents_v4_1.md:1155`). Generates C1, C4, C7, and the citation pattern in C12.
- *Alternative A (bilevel/end-to-end):* train the encoder against a differentiable validation-fold covariance or entropy loss, with the ELBO as a regulariser. Trade-off: noisy, low-signal gradients and a real risk of overfitting the validation fold — but it is the only variant that trains the *thing being scored*.
- *Alternative B (decouple but align):* keep the generative objective; change the *selection* rule to a proxy-robust scoring rule on the risk model (QLIKE on $\Sigma_z$, Patton 2011 `[unverified]`). Trade-off: requires a validation horizon with several independent windows — precisely what C1 says the current design lacks.
- *Alternative C (multi-task head):* add a cross-sectional covariance/factor-return head trained jointly, so the latent geometry is shaped by risk structure. Trade-off: more parameters against ~15 effective windows per stock.

**R2 — Calendar and structural constants chosen for convenience, rationalised afterwards.** 2 years, 100 epochs, 10/5 patience, 256 batch, 1e-4 LR, monthly-quarterly retraining. Generates C1, C3, C7, C8, C10, C12. The tell is that each constant carries a confident but non-derivable justification ("standard for comparable architectures", "conservative").
- *Alternative A:* derive each constant from a stated constraint (validation span must contain ≥ 5-10 independent T-windows; LR from a pre-registered sensitivity sweep). Trade-off: forces admitting how little validation data exists.
- *Alternative B:* replace epoch counts with criterion-based triggers defined relative to the schedule, and report a sensitivity surface rather than a point value. Trade-off: more runs, more reported variance — which is the point.

**R3 — Coupled schedules with no termination semantics.** The curriculum needs a horizon; the stopping rule sets the horizon; the curriculum changes the loss the stopping rule reads. Generates C2, C8, C9. This is a control problem disguised as a hyperparameter table.
- *Alternative A:* make λ_co a function of a measured signal (saturation of Spearman(latent distance, realised correlation) on a held-out block) rather than of epochs. Trade-off: another early-stopping-like criterion, but one defined on the object that matters.
- *Alternative B:* stage the training — Phase 1-2 to a fixed, pre-registered horizon, then Phase 3 as a separate run with its own stopping rule. Trade-off: two models, one extra selection step.
- *Alternative C:* drop the curriculum entirely and fold the relational signal into a permanent, well-scaled loss term (the InfoNCE direction already sketched at `:1149-1157`). Trade-off: reintroduces the linear-geometry bias the curriculum was invented to escape.

**R4 — Non-stationarity asserted away.** "Uniform across all history" plus "current relevance at inference" is a claim that the pooled distribution is the right learning target; §6.2 contradicts it, γ contradicts it, and the mean aggregation cannot express it. Generates C5, C10, C11.
- *Alternative A (conditional VAE):* loadings produced by a network conditioned on a low-dimensional recent-state vector; trained pooled, evaluated on OOS covariance loss. Trade-off: sample-hungry per regime — but this is the clearest capability PCA cannot express, and thus the sharpest test of the deep-learning premise.
- *Alternative B (continual training with a stability penalty):* anchor parameters across retrainings (EWC-like `[unverified]`), keeping dimension identity. Trade-off: a new hyperparameter and a plausible under-reaction after structural breaks.
- *Alternative C (learned aggregation, promoted from Iteration 7):* move relevance weighting into the composite profile where it is measured, instead of into the gradient where it is invisible. Trade-off: another trainable module and a new overfitting channel on ~15 effective windows.

*On the "would a linear model suffice?" question (not an admissible conclusion, but a required test):* PCA + Ledoit-Wolf is a strong baseline and the corpus is honest about it (`:1092-1097`). The defensible deep-learning claim is not "non-linearity" in the abstract but state-conditional exposures and learned aggregation. The refuting experiment is pre-registered, seed-averaged and margin-based: if the VAE's advantage over PCA on OOS $\Sigma_z$ QLIKE and portfolio entropy is not larger than the across-seed dispersion of the VAE itself (C6), the deep component has demonstrated nothing — which is a statement about the *evidence*, not about the architecture.

## Verdict

This is the weakest layer of an otherwise unusually self-aware protocol. §4.5 is where the theory's two load-bearing claims — all-history memory and crisis emphasis — are supposed to become an algorithm, and both conversions fail. The convergence criterion rests on a validation block that is exactly one window long (C1); the curriculum that is supposed to make the latent geometry relational is clocked by the stopping rule it should precede, and in the deployed run it ends at a different schedule coordinate than the checkpoint it was validated at (C2); the checkpoint criterion deliberately cancels the crisis reweighting the loss introduces (C4); and none of it is replicated (C6). The supporting hyperparameters are internally contradictory (C3), justified by citation rather than derivation (C12), and the effective batch size implied by the document's own formula is an order of magnitude below the one the learning rate was chosen for (C9). Because these are structural — a proxy objective, a coupled-schedule control problem, and a refusal to model non-stationarity — they will not be fixed by tightening constants; they require moving model selection to the risk layer and giving the curriculum a horizon independent of the stopping rule.

Score: 3/10
