## Five questions

**1. Purpose and original intent.** "The validation protocol answers whether the strategy *actually* works without hindsight" (`DVT_strategie_facteurs_risque_latents_v4_1.md:862`); the walk-forward is meant to simulate deployment, provide "a distribution of OOS results across ~34 folds" (`:866`), select HPs in Phase A, deploy in Phase B, and reserve a final holdout as "the only uncontaminated evaluation" (`:880`). The philosophy elevates this to a terminal principle: the VAE must "statistically outperform the PCA factor risk parity baseline across walk-forward folds" or be abandoned (`strategy_philosophy.md:183`).

**2. What a state-of-the-art version would look like.** Not "more folds", but (i) a *nested* design in which the sample used to choose is disjoint from the sample used to estimate performance, with one outer evaluation per configuration; (ii) explicit control of the search burden: Bailey & López de Prado (2014), *The Deflated Sharpe Ratio* (selection-bias and non-normality correction as a function of the number of trials) [unverified]; Bailey, Borwein, López de Prado & Zhu (2016), *The Probability of Backtest Overfitting* (CSCV estimate of PBO from the configuration matrix) [unverified]; White (2000), *A Reality Check for Data Snooping* and Hansen (2005), *A Test for Superior Predictive Ability* (bootstrap tests that account for the full set of candidates) [unverified]; (iii) inference that respects dependence: Diebold & Mariano (1995) on HAC-adjusted loss differentials and Politis & Romano (1994) *The Stationary Bootstrap* for serially dependent series [unverified]; (iv) purging/embargo derived from the label-overlap horizon rather than a fixed 21 days (López de Prado 2018, *Advances in Financial Machine Learning*, purged K-fold and combinatorial purged CV) [unverified]; (v) proper scoring rules for the covariance (=the model's own object) as a selection signal rather than portfolio outcomes only (Patton 2011, *Volatility forecast comparison using imperfect volatility proxies*) [unverified]; (vi) stochasticity treated as part of the estimator (Lakshminarayanan, Pritzel & Blundell 2017, deep ensembles, as a variance estimate) [unverified]. A pre-registration discipline is spelled out by Arnott, Harvey & Markowitz (2019), *A Backtesting Protocol in the Era of Machine Learning* [unverified].

**3. What prevents it.** F01 (selection on the evaluation sample), F02 (two different objects under one label), F03 (score not computable/not comparable across folds), F04 (the primary metric is capacity-biased), F05 (surrogate selection vs deployed model), F07 (invalid inference on correlated folds), F08 (no seed dispersion).

**4. Real vs self-imposed constraints.** Real: one realized price history; non-stationarity; an unsupervised latent object with no ground truth; compute for a 32-config grid; vendor delisted coverage "since 2000" against a 30-year requirement (`DVT_strategie_facteurs_risque_latents_v4_1.md:291`) with no CRSP. Self-imposed: per-fold HP re-selection; a 6-month frozen portfolio; expanding-only windows; a 21-day embargo chosen although the corpus itself admits the range is 21–126 days (`:1058`) and that there is "zero shared daily returns … even without embargo" (`:876`); a weighted-sum score with three free constants; a single configuration carried into the holdout; one training run per cell; treating 34 folds as 34 draws.

**5. What should be removed, merged or moved.** Remove per-fold HP re-selection (it converts the backtest into the record of an adaptive policy); remove λ_pen and λ_est from the selection score and replace them with lexicographic gates; move Layer-2 model-quality metrics from "targets" into the *selection* stage, so that the model is selected on its own object (covariance forecast loss) and the portfolio is evaluated once; remove the 6-month frozen-weight simulation and align the fold clock with the specified rebalancing clock; merge the two holdout paragraphs (`DVT…:880` and `Benchmark…:283`) into one rule with a pre-declared effect size and decision; delete the "34-fold distribution" claim and replace it with an effective-sample-size statement plus a block resampling scheme; delete the EP/MSE duplication with two different floors (F12).

**Steelman (to be rebutted).** The design is unusually self-aware for a strategy paper: it separates the encoder's validation set from the config comparison, it fixes the embargo and the fold dates before evaluation, it declines to re-optimise constraints per benchmark (`Benchmark_latent_risk_factor.md:48`), it writes the point-in-time constraint as an invariant (`ISD_vae_latent_risk_factors.md:2349–2351`), it excludes γ and λ_co from the validation ELBO precisely because their inclusion would bias fold selection (`:163–166`), and the decision log catches the global-median E* as a look-ahead and replaces it with an expanding median (`implementation_decisions.md:311–320`). The intent is evidently to be honest. **Rebuttal:** every one of these measures polices the *boundary* between train and test while leaving the *selection loop* inside the test period; the document's own sentence — "computed on data entirely unseen during training and early stopping" (`DVT_…:620`, repeated at `:929`) — substitutes "unseen during fitting" for "unseen during selection", which is the one distinction that matters.

## Root-cause hypotheses

**RC1 — Selection pressure with no model-level objective (F01, F02, F05, F09).** Because the latent object is unsupervised, nothing in the theory can score a *risk model* except the portfolio it produces. Layer 2 exists (`DVT_…:910`) but is diagnostic; the only decision signal is the OOS portfolio score. All judgment therefore collapses onto the test set, and every new knob (K, α, φ, κ) is necessarily fitted there. Symptoms across four units (U06, U12, U13, U14) share this cause. Structural alternatives: (a) promote covariance forecast loss (QLIKE-style) to the selection signal and evaluate the portfolio once — trade-off: abandons the entropy objective as a selection criterion and requires defending a predictive loss for a shape-only model; (b) a genuinely nested design with an inner selection fold and an outer evaluation fold, one config per outer fold — trade-off: ~17 outer folds instead of 34, i.e. less power; (c) accept the object as a *meta-strategy* whose policy is part of the theory, evaluate only on the holdout — trade-off: honest but says nothing about the deep model per se.

**RC2 — Scalarization with free exchange rates (F03, F04, F12).** A single weighted sum is used to compare an information-theoretic quantity, a tail realisation and a parameter-count diagnostic, none of which share units; the weights are "baselines". The normalization step then tries to repair a dimensional problem with a monotone transform, which introduces a new bias rather than removing the old one. Alternatives: (a) lexicographic gates (feasibility → forecast accuracy → entropy) with no weights — trade-off: no compensating trade-offs, some good configs get rejected; (b) report the Pareto frontier over (entropy, MDD, R_Σ) and never select — trade-off: no single winner, a decision rule is still needed; (c) replace Ĥ by an *attainable* normalization, (H − H_min)/(H_max(constraints) − H_min), where H_max is the entropy-only optimum under the same constraints — trade-off: one extra optimisation per fold, and H_min remains a convention.

**RC3 — A stochastic model administered as a deterministic one (F05, F08).** One run, one checkpoint, one epoch count, one configuration. The theory's own object (a distribution over latent encodings) is discarded at the evaluation boundary. Alternatives: (a) make the seed ensemble the estimator and report its dispersion — trade-off: S× compute, and the deployed object becomes an ensemble; (b) a variance decomposition of the fold score (seed/fold/config) with selection on seed-averaged scores — trade-off: still one history; (c) propagate a posterior over B and Σ_z into w (deep ensemble or Bayesian last layer) and evaluate the resulting distribution of entropy — trade-off: changes what "the portfolio" means and complicates every downstream comparison.

**RC4 — Ritual hygiene instead of causal hygiene (F06, F10, F11, and the embargo).** Rigour is spent on artefacts that look like protection — embargo length, expanding windows, "34 folds", invariants, deterministic epochs — while the material contamination sits in the selection loop and the material mismatch sits between the backtest clock and the deployment clock. The decision log catching the global-median E* (`implementation_decisions.md:311–320`) is the tell: the point-in-time rule was asserted, not checked, and only an implementer's objection enforced it. Alternatives: (a) a data-provenance ledger mapping each parameter to the data allowed to influence it (forces the admission that α and κ were chosen after seeing results); (b) one clock for backtest and deployment, with fold boundaries only marking refits — trade-off: the 6-month OOS blocks stop being usable evaluation units; (c) replace fold counting with an effective-sample-size statement plus block resampling.

**RC5 — One-path epistemology (F07, F11).** A single 30-year US path is treated as a sample of 34 independent draws. Time-series folds measure parameter stability, not generalisation; the theory's claims are about market structure, which needs a second market, a second universe or a known-truth simulator to be tested at all. Alternatives: (a) block bootstrap over dates to construct alternative histories — trade-off: destroys cross-sectional structure unless resampled jointly; (b) cross-market or size-segment replication as primary evidence — trade-off: new data requirements; (c) a calibrated simulator with known latent structure, to test whether the protocol can recover B at all before asking whether it beats PCA — trade-off: tests the estimator, not the market.

## Verdict

The unit is well-organised and unusually explicit about its own mechanics, but its central inferential claim is false: every fold's reported out-of-sample metric is the maximum of a 32-point search performed on that same fold, structural HPs are chosen using all folds and applied retroactively, and the score used to choose is not the score reported, is undefined at the level at which it is applied, and is dominated at early folds by a diagnostic term whose weight drifts with sample size. The two-layer Phase A/Phase B structure does not fix this — it addresses train/serve mismatch, a different problem, and silently substitutes a surrogate model for the one that was selected. The consequences propagate: the primary metric is capacity-biased against exactly the high-AU configurations the theory is built to find, so the VAE-versus-PCA comparison has no power in the direction that matters; the inference on 34 correlated, censored fold scores is invalid; and the one clean sample (the holdout) carries no decision rule while the go/no-go rule sits on the contaminated sample. What survives is worth keeping: the fold/embargo scaffolding, the exclusion of γ and λ_co from the validation ELBO, the point-in-time universe discipline, and the insistence on a benchmark ladder. What must go is the selection loop inside the evaluation period, the scalarized score, and the six-month freeze that makes the backtest a simulation of a different strategy. The redesign this unit needs is not a better metric but a different topology: model selection on the model's own object, portfolio evaluation once per configuration, and a holdout that decides.

Score: 3/10
