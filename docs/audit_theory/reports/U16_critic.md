## Five questions

**1. Purpose and original intent.** The unit exists to make the complexity of the deep model *justified or condemned by evidence*: "la complexité du VAE n'est justifiée que si elle produit un bénéfice OOS mesurable et statistiquement significatif par rapport à des alternatives plus simples" (`Benchmark_latent_risk_factor.md:5`), with a stated hierarchy of metrics that "déterminent la décision" (`Benchmark_latent_risk_factor.md:168`), paired fold tests with Holm correction (`Benchmark_latent_risk_factor.md:207-224`), a regime split whose failure "invalide l'hypothèse fondamentale" (`Benchmark_latent_risk_factor.md:228`), a one-shot holdout (`Benchmark_latent_risk_factor.md:277-283`), and a five-scenario action grid (`Benchmark_latent_risk_factor.md:287-311`, replicated verbatim in `ISD_vae_latent_risk_factors.md:2697-2705`).

**2. What a state-of-the-art version would look like.** Three things are missing, all with canonical answers.
- *Selection-vs-inference separation*: the evaluation sample must be disjoint from the sample used to choose any configuration; otherwise the reported statistic is a maximum of correlated draws. Nested walk-forward with an inner/outer fold hierarchy is the standard fix; White (2000), "A Reality Check for Data Snooping", *Econometrica* 68(5), and Hansen (2005), "A Test for Superior Predictive Ability", *JBES* 23(4), both define the null as "no model in the family beats the benchmark" and bootstrap the max-statistic — exactly the object this protocol needs and does not have [unverified].
- *Dependence-aware inference*: Diebold & Mariano (1995), "Comparing Predictive Accuracy", *JBES* 13(3), tests equal loss with a HAC correction for serial dependence in the loss differential; Politis & Romano (1994), "The Stationary Bootstrap", *JASA* 89(428), gives the block resampling counterpart [unverified]. A single 30-year path with 34 adjacent 6-month folds is not 34 i.i.d. experiments.
- *Equivalence and effect floors*: a non-rejection is not equivalence. Schuirmann (1987), TOST [unverified]; and for the multiplicity of analyst choices, Harvey, Liu & Zhu (2016), "…and the Cross-Section of Expected Returns", *RFS* 29(1), argue a t-threshold near 3 rather than 1.96 after accounting for the number of trials [unverified]. For covariance forecasts specifically, the loss must be a proper scoring rule against a noisy proxy — Patton (2011), "Volatility forecast comparison using imperfect volatility proxies", *J. Econometrics* 160(1), shows QLIKE and MSE are robust to proxy noise while MAE/others are not [unverified].
The corpus already cites Meucci (2009) "Managing Diversification" for effective number of bets (`DVT_strategie_facteurs_risque_latents_v4_1.md:1654`) and Choueifaty & Coignard (2008) (`benchmark:336`), i.e. it has the references for a basis-free diversification metric in hand and does not use them for the comparison.

**3. What prevents it from reaching that.** Not a missing tool — a structural conflation. The same 34 folds are simultaneously (i) the hyperparameter selection sample (`DVT_strategie_facteurs_risque_latents_v4_1.md:927-929`), (ii) the benchmark penalty-calibration sample (`Benchmark_latent_risk_factor.md:48`), (iii) the α-elbow calibration sample (`Benchmark_latent_risk_factor.md:144`), and (iv) the inferential sample (`Benchmark_latent_risk_factor.md:211-216`). Layered on top: the primary metric is defined in each model's *own* factor basis with a model-chosen denominator (`Benchmark_latent_risk_factor.md:172`), and the decision rule is a conjunction over six arms with no effect-size floor (`Benchmark_latent_risk_factor.md:293`). *Steelman*: the design is unusually honest for a strategy document — a paired design, a non-parametric test, an FWER correction, six nested benchmarks each ablating one stage (`Benchmark_latent_risk_factor.md:315-324`), an IQR instead of a mean, a fold-sensitivity check (`DVT_strategie_facteurs_risque_latents_v4_1.md:959`), and a permanent holdout. *Rebuttal*: every one of those strengths is neutralised by the four-way reuse of one sample and by a metric whose denominator is free. Honest machinery applied to a contaminated sample produces precise-looking nonsense, and Holm's validity under arbitrary dependence (`Benchmark_latent_risk_factor.md:224`) does not repair a biased test statistic.

**4. Real vs self-imposed constraints.** Real: 30 years is one macro path; ~5-8 independent business cycles is a hard ceiling on regime-level effective sample size; a 3-year holdout has 6 folds and cannot support inference. Self-imposed: fold-level testing as the inferential unit (daily or monthly loss differentials are available and far more numerous, at the cost of dependence — which they then must handle anyway); a single composite score mixing a utilization ratio and a drawdown penalty; the choice to *exclude* the holdout from testing (`ISD_vae_latent_risk_factors.md:2578`); binary crisis/calm labels instead of a continuous regime exposure; and the demand that the VAE beat *all six* benchmarks rather than a designated primary comparator.

**5. What should be removed, merged or moved.** *Remove*: the fold-level Wilcoxon signed-rank test on 34 adjacent folds (replace with a dependence-aware loss-differential test or block bootstrap); the composite score of `DVT_strategie_facteurs_risque_latents_v4_1.md:933` (its two terms are incommensurable and its weights are unargued); Scenario E as a *decision* (it is an observation, and the prescribed action "dual-regime system" is a new theory, not a decision); the entropy metric from the primary family. *Merge*: the two decision surfaces (composite score vs five-scenario grid) into one lexicographic rule; the four "primary" metrics into one primary (realized covariance accuracy or realized portfolio variance) plus a hard CVaR-of-fold-MDD constraint; Benchmark §3.3 into the decision family rather than a diagnostic annex. *Move*: the holdout from "reported separately" to the only inferential sample, with all selection confined to the walk-forward; the α elbow calibration out of the evaluation loop into a pre-registered fixed value.

**U16-CRI-01, in full.** Let $c_k$ be the risk contributions normalized to sum to one over the model's reported factor set. $\hat H = -\sum c_k\ln c_k / \ln(\text{AU})$ is a ratio whose denominator is chosen by the model. Take a VAE with AU = 85 whose portfolio has entropy $H = 2.303$ nats: $\hat H = 2.303/4.443 = 0.518$. Take PCA with $k=10$ whose portfolio has the *same* absolute entropy $H = 2.303$: $\hat H = 2.303/2.303 = 1.000$. Identical diversification, a 1.93× score gap, purely from $\ln(85)/\ln(10)$. Because PCA's entropy is capped at $\ln 10$ while the VAE's cap is $\ln 85$, PCA reaches $\hat H = 1$ whenever its ten factors are equi-contributing, whereas the VAE needs near-perfect equi-contribution across 85. Second, truncation is free: drop the 45 smallest-contribution factors. $H$ barely changes (their contribution is negligible) while the denominator falls from $\ln 85 = 4.443$ to $\ln 40 = 3.689$, raising $\hat H$ by ≈17% with a *literally unchanged portfolio*. The VAE is truncated to $\text{AU}^{\text{stat}}_{\max}$ (`DVT_…:436`) whereas PCA's $k$ comes from Bai-Ng IC₂ (`Benchmark_…:137`) — so the primary metric measures truncation policy. Third, the fix at `DVT_…:937-939` does not work: normalizing by the model's own capacity converts an absolute quantity into a relative one whose denominator is free, replacing a monotone AU bias with an inverse one. The example at `DVT_…:943-951` demonstrates the defect: config B has $\bar H = 4.20 >$ config A's $3.80$ — objectively 11% more diversification in nats — and is nonetheless scored lower. Fourth, "non applicable" for 1/N and inverse-vol makes "beats the 6 benchmarks on ≥2 of 4 primary metrics" (`Benchmark_…:293`) undefined on the entropy dimension for two arms, while those two arms can still win the entropy dimension against PCA. The rule therefore applies a different bar per benchmark and cannot be evaluated as written.

**U16-CRI-02, in full.** Let $S_j^{(k)}$ be the fold-$k$ score of config $j$, $j=1..m$, $m\in[18,32]$ (`ISD_…:2477-2481`; `implementation_decisions.md:186`). Reported observation: $\hat S^{(k)}=\max_j S_j^{(k)}$. If the configs were exchangeable with mean $\mu_k$ and sd $\sigma_k$, $\mathbb{E}[\hat S^{(k)}]\geq \mu_k + \sigma_k\,\Phi^{-1}(0.5^{1/m})$; for $m=32$, $\Phi^{-1}(0.5^{1/32}) = \Phi^{-1}(0.9786)\approx 2.03$. So the VAE arm's per-fold score is inflated by ≈2 $\sigma_k$ relative to its own population mean, while each benchmark arm is a single deterministic model per fold with no such inflation. This bias enters $\Delta_k$ directly and no multiplicity correction removes it, because the test treats the selected config as pre-registered. The document's own defense — "computed on data unseen during both training and early stopping" (`DVT_…:929`) — establishes that the OOS is unseen *by the model*, not *by the analyst*; `:894` states the same OOS score selects the config. Structural hyperparameters are worse: "coarse grid across full walk-forward" (`:927`), i.e. one config chosen by its average over the entire 17-year evaluation span, whose per-fold scores are then tested. Meanwhile the only clean data, the 3-year holdout, is explicitly removed from every test (`ISD_…:2578`) — the protocol reserves clean data and then declines to draw inference from it. Both error directions follow: a "VAE wins" result is uninterpretable, and a "PCA ≈ VAE" result is forced.

**U16-CRI-03, in full.** The signed-rank statistic's null distribution is derived under exchangeability of the $K$ signed ranks. Here fold $k$'s OOS block is adjacent to fold $k+1$'s, and fold $k+1$'s training set strictly contains fold $k$'s (`DVT_…:886-890`), so model errors and market regime are shared across neighbours: $\mathrm{corr}(\Delta_k,\Delta_{k+1})=\rho>0$ is the generic case, not the exception. For the mean $\bar\Delta$ used implicitly by any location test, $\mathrm{Var}(\bar\Delta)=\frac{\sigma^2}{K}\cdot\frac{1+\rho}{1-\rho}$ for AR(1). At $\rho=0.4$ the inflation factor is $\sqrt{1.4/0.6}=1.53$; a nominal two-sided $\alpha=0.05$ ($z=1.96$) then corresponds to a true $z=1.28$, i.e. an actual size of ≈0.20. At $\rho=0.5$ the factor is 1.73 and the actual size ≈0.26. A rank test does not repair this: it is distribution-free, not dependence-free. Worse, the economically meaningful unit is the macro episode, not the fold: 30 years contain roughly 5-8 expansions/recessions, so the effective sample for any regime-contingent claim is single-digit, and the ~34-fold framing overstates the independent sample by a factor of 4-6. The correct objects are a loss differential $d_t$ at daily/monthly frequency tested with a HAC variance (Diebold-Mariano) or a block bootstrap, and a max-statistic over the model family (White's Reality Check / Hansen's SPA) that puts the VAE and the six benchmarks in the *same* null.

**U16-CRI-07, in full.** A 6-month fold is ≈126 trading days (`Benchmark_…:56`). An expanding-window 80th VIX percentile implies asymptotically 20% of days exceed it, so crisis days per fold are $\mathrm{Bin}(126,0.2)$: mean 25.2, sd $\sqrt{126\times0.2\times0.8}=\sqrt{20.16}\approx4.49$, i.e. 3.6% of days. The classification threshold ">20% of days" is $>25.2$ days — zero standard deviations above the mean. Hence $\Pr(\text{fold labelled crisis})\approx0.5$ *by sampling noise alone*, independent of stress intensity: about half of all folds are "crisis" in a period with no crisis at all, and a genuinely severe fold is not distinguished from a mildly above-average one. The label therefore cannot support the inference it is assigned ("si elle ne le fait que sur les folds calmes, l'hypothèse fondamentale est invalidée", `:228`). Two further defects compound it: the split is deterministic given the calendar, so it can be known ex ante and invites post-hoc interpretation; and each cell then gets ~17 pairs against 24 tests (`:275`), pushing the cell-wise Holm threshold to ~0.002 on 17 dependent observations.

## Root-cause hypotheses

**RC-A — Evaluation lives on the selection sample (one path, four uses).** Generates CRI-02, CRI-04, CRI-09, and half of CRI-03. Every honest device in the unit (paired design, Holm, bootstrap CI, fold-sensitivity) is applied to data that has already been used to choose the model, the α, and the benchmark penalties. *Structural alternatives:* (a) **inner/outer walk-forward** — selection strictly inside an inner fold hierarchy, inference only on outer folds, which are never scored during selection; trade-off is a 2-3× widening of the compute and a shorter inferential span, and the theory must accept that Scenario A may become undecidable on the available history. (b) **Pre-registration** — one configuration, fixed α, fixed penalties, declared before the holdout; the holdout becomes the sole inferential sample with a sequential test so α inflation is controlled by construction rather than by correction. (c) **Family-wise max-statistic** — keep the selection but redefine the null as "no model in $\{VAE\}\cup\{6\ \text{benchmarks}\}$ beats the best benchmark", bootstrapped over folds (White/Hansen); this absorbs the selection bias into the null instead of pretending it is absent.

**RC-B — Metrics are expressed in each model's own representation.** Generates CRI-01, CRI-05, CRI-06, CRI-11, and the α-elbow tie. Entropy over one's own factors, and α calibrated on one's own frontier, cannot compare two models; and because only Layer 2 touches the covariance, the unit never separates *risk-model accuracy* from *optimizer behaviour*. This is the root cause of the whole unit being unable to attribute anything. *Structural alternatives:* (a) **common-basis evaluation** — score every portfolio's risk concentration on a fixed external basis (a reference covariance or the principal portfolios of the realized OOS covariance, the Meucci ENB already cited at `DVT_…:1654`), so all arms are measured in the same coordinates; trade-off: the metric then partially rewards the reference model, so the reference must be a neutral, pre-specified object (e.g. the sample covariance of the OOS window, used only for evaluation). (b) **Frozen-optimizer ablation** — hold the optimizer, α and constraints identical across arms and feed it each model's Σ only, so any difference is attributable to the risk model; trade-off: it discards each model's own natural allocation and may understate a model whose advantage *is* its factor basis. (c) **Representation-free predictive loss** — make realized-versus-predicted covariance a proper scoring rule (QLIKE/MSE-type on the portfolio-variance and correlation blocks) the primary metric; trade-off: it tests the risk model, not the strategy, so the economic decision still needs a second, separate criterion.

**RC-C — Inference machinery imported from i.i.d. experimental statistics onto a single dependent path.** Generates CRI-03, CRI-07, A1, A8. *Structural alternatives:* (a) **loss-differential time series with HAC/block bootstrap** (Diebold-Mariano style), using the daily or monthly loss rather than the fold aggregate; trade-off: overlapping portfolio horizons require Newey-West with a long bandwidth, and power is still limited by ~5-8 macro episodes. (b) **Episode-level analysis** — collapse to non-overlapping 2-3 year blocks aligned to recessions and report the full distribution of block outcomes plus a sign test, with an explicit statement that $n\approx6-8$; trade-off: honest but low-power, and the theory must then accept "undetermined" as a legitimate verdict. (c) **Continuous regime conditioning** — replace the binary crisis label with a continuous stress exposure (mean VIX percentile rank in the fold) and fit a regime-interaction on the loss differential; trade-off: introduces a functional form and the risk of overfitting the regime axis, which is exactly what the current binary split was trying to avoid.

**RC-D — Decision rule specifies actions but not magnitudes, equivalences or ordering.** Generates CRI-04, CRI-08, CRI-09, CRI-10. Five named scenarios with hand-written actions and a competing composite score mean the rule can be reinterpreted after the fact; no effect floor, no equivalence band, no lexicographic priority, and inconsistent thresholds. *Structural alternatives:* (a) **Lexicographic rule** — hard constraints (explanatory power, AU guard, covariance-forecast error) first, then a *single* declared primary with a pre-registered minimum economically meaningful effect, then tiebreak by cost; trade-off: discards the composite's flexibility and forces the theory to commit to one number in advance. (b) **TOST equivalence band** — declare "PCA is at least as good" only if the confidence interval lies inside a pre-specified band on the primary; trade-off: requires an economic judgement about the band that the corpus currently avoids. (c) **Value-of-complexity curve** — report performance as a function of effective capacity AU (or of parameter count) for all arms and locate the VAE on the curve instead of labelling a scenario; trade-off: harder to communicate as a go/no-go, but it removes goal-post moving and makes "the deep model adds value at *some* capacity" a testable statement rather than a binary.

**Cross-unit note for the synthesis.** The single most consequential structural move is RC-B: the protocol currently asks "does the deep model produce a better *portfolio* under an optimizer tuned to erase differences", when the falsifiable claim of the theory is "the deep model produces a better *risk structure*". A deep-learning formulation that a linear model cannot match would have to earn its keep on a representation-free predictive loss — e.g. a latent factor model whose conditional covariance beats LW/PCA specifically in the tail or in regime transitions, tested by a covariance scoring rule evaluated separately in crisis and calm *continuous* stress strata, with the optimizer frozen.
