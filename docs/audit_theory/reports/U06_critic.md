## Five questions

**1. Purpose and original intent.** Remove the human choice of the number of risk factors. `DVT_strategie_facteurs_risque_latents_v4_1.md:402` states "K is a **capacity ceiling**, not the number of factors the model will use"; `:406` says "the effective factor count is read as AU after training"; `strategy_philosophy.md:75` says "The number of risk factors is not chosen in advance"; `:82` claims "the strategy does not require a human decision about how many risk factors exist in the market". A secondary intent is downstream estimability: the guard at `DVT_strategie_facteurs_risque_latents_v4_1.md:422-436` exists because `AU × AU` covariance has `AU(AU+1)/2` parameters. Operationally, `ISD_vae_latent_risk_factors.md:51` fixes AU "determined once per retraining".

**2. What a state-of-the-art version would look like.** The number of latent factors must be a *functional of an identified object*, selected by a criterion that penalizes complexity and measures out-of-sample fit. Canonical elements: (i) a consistent information criterion — Bai & Ng (2002), *Determining the Number of Factors in Approximate Factor Models*, Econometrica: IC₁/IC₂/IC₃ with penalty increasing in `k`, consistent as `n,T→∞` [unverified]; (ii) an eigenvalue-gap test with a null distribution — Onatski (2010), *Determining the Number of Factors from Empirical Distribution of Eigenvalues*, REStat [unverified]; (iii) identifiability of the latent code before any coordinate is named — Khemakhem, Kingma, Monti & Hyvärinen (2020), *Variational Autoencoders and Nonlinear ICA*, AISTATS: latent variables identifiable up to permutation and linear mixing given an auxiliary variable [unverified]; Locatello et al. (2019), *Challenging Common Assumptions in the Unsupervised Learning of Disentangled Representations*, ICML: coordinate-wise disentanglement is not identifiable without inductive bias [unverified]; (iv) the active-unit statistic used only as a *posterior-collapse* diagnostic at the scale of Burda, Grosse & Salakhutdinov (2016), *Importance Weighted Autoencoders*, ICLR, where the 0.01-nat convention originates [unverified]; and (v) selection of the factor count on a *forecast loss* (covariance or portfolio-variance loss), not on a normalization of the optimizer's own output — in the spirit of Fleming, Kirby & Ostdiek (2003), *The Economic Value of Volatility Timing*, JF [unverified]. A SOTA unit would report the factor spectrum with a noise band, and would define "number of factors" as the smallest `m` whose OOS covariance loss is statistically indistinguishable from the loss at `m+1`.

**3. What prevents the current unit from reaching it.** `U06-CRI-01` (the count is a coordinate statistic on an unidentified basis), `U06-CRI-02` (the count is set by the sample-size formula the document itself calls binding), `U06-CRI-03` (selection metric is scale-free in AU and the only penalty is disabled), `U06-MAJ-05` (capacity ratio counts overlapping windows), `U06-MAJ-06` (PCA comparison confounded by factor count), `U06-MAJ-09` (selection criterion unrelated to the criterion that uses the factors).

**4. Real constraints vs self-imposed choices.** Real: `Σ_z` must be estimable from the available dates and the cross-sectional OLS needs `n > AU` per date (`DVT_strategie_facteurs_risque_latents_v4_1.md:718`); sample eigenvalues are biased; the universe is fixed by a non-CRSP vendor. Self-imposed: the 0.01-nat threshold as a *count* (`:404`); marginal KL as the ranking statistic for truncation (`:436`); hard truncation rather than the ridge already present downstream (`ISD_vae_latent_risk_factors.md:1746`); `r_min = 2` (`DVT…:426`); `K = 200` and `K ∈ {100,200}` (`implementation_decisions.md:171`); selecting K on `Ĥ = H/ln(AU)` (`DVT…:933-937`); requiring `AU ≥ AU_PCA` (`:473`); treating AU as the entropy dimension (`:690`).

**5. What should be removed, merged or moved.** *Remove*: the 0.01-nat AU criterion as a factor-count estimator (retain it only as a collapse alarm, `ISD…:67`); the `AU_max_stat` hard truncation (the shrinkage/ridge machinery and a ridge guard already exist, `ISD…:1752`, `:1746`); the "noise absorption buffer" rationale (`DVT…:420`); the `K ∈ {AU, 1.5AU, 2AU}` grid (`:418`) — K and AU are not independent knobs once the guard binds. *Merge*: the two competing factor-count definitions (AU and `AU_max_stat`) into one risk-model dimension chosen by an OOS covariance loss; the DVT and ISD closed forms for that dimension (`:424` vs `ISD…:82`). *Move*: the decision of "how many factors are used" out of the encoder and into the risk model — the VAE produces a K-dimensional representation, the covariance estimator decides how many directions are estimable; and move factor characterization into the principal basis (after `Σ_z = VΛVᵀ`) rather than the raw latent coordinates.

## Root-cause hypotheses

**RC1 — Second-moment collapse (drives CRI-01, CRI-03, MAJ-06, MAJ-09).** The theory only ever consumes the latent code through a covariance (`Σ_z`, then `λ_k`); every question about the latent space is thereby reduced to a variance-ordering question. Hence the factor count is a variance-like statistic, the PCA comparison is dimensionally confounded, and the only distinguishable property of a factor is its eigenvalue. *Alternatives:* (a) make the network output a **conditional** covariance (regime- and tail-dependent `Σ_z`), so the factor count is defined per regime and cannot be reproduced by a single linear model — trade-off: fewer effective observations per regime, sharper estimation error; (b) learn the **conditional density** and select the count by out-of-sample likelihood rather than reconstruction — trade-off: harder optimization, less interpretable than a factor model; (c) learn a nonlinear **factor-return mapping** and test for nonlinearity in that mapping — trade-off: reintroduces return views the philosophy deliberately excludes (`DVT…:680`).

**RC2 — Unidentified representation treated as identified (drives CRI-01, MAJ-08).** The VAE latent basis is not identified, yet the pipeline derives counts, truncation, breadth, persistence and economic interpretation from coordinates. The corpus already contains the correct fix in one place — the rotation-invariant pairwise-distance stability metric (`DVT…:908`) — and then does not apply the same principle anywhere else. *Alternatives:* (a) define every factor-level quantity on rotation-invariant functionals (subspace distance, spectrum, risk contributions) — trade-off: loses the ability to name individual factors; (b) impose identifiability by construction (auxiliary-variable/time-contrastive VAE) so coordinates mean something — trade-off: requires an auxiliary variable (regime label, time index) and changes the model; (c) operate only in the principal basis and report no raw-coordinate statistic — trade-off: the principal basis is fold-specific, so no cross-fold factor identity either.

**RC3 — A single free dial tuned by the criterion it defines (drives CRI-02, CRI-03, MAJ-10, MAJ-07).** K, `AU_max_stat`, `r_min` and the 0.01-nat threshold are all adjusted until the downstream pipeline has the capacity the designer wants, while the score that judges the result is normalized by the same quantity. The unit is protected from every direction by a ring of redundant mechanisms (generous K, AU pruning, statistical guard, truncation, elimination thresholds). *Alternatives:* (a) fix the risk-model dimension by an OOS covariance loss with an explicit complexity penalty — trade-off: needs a covariance benchmark and a loss function the theory does not currently define; (b) pre-register the count once on a validation block and freeze it — trade-off: no adaptation, but it makes the claim falsifiable; (c) delete the redundant layers and keep one criterion — trade-off: some folds will show the model is mis-sized, which is the point.

**RC4 — The count is asked to do two incompatible jobs (drives CRI-02, MAJ-09, MAJ-06).** AU must simultaneously be (i) a discovered property of the data, (ii) the dimension of `Σ_z` that is estimable, and (iii) the number of bins in the diversification objective. No single statistic can serve all three, so each constraint is bent to satisfy the others. *Alternatives:* (a) decouple representation dimension (K, a model-capacity choice) from risk-model dimension (m, an estimation choice) from allocation dimension (the number of contributions the optimizer equalizes) — trade-off: three numbers to justify instead of one; (b) let the optimizer's stability requirement set m via a condition-number target on `B̃ᵀB̃` — trade-off: m becomes fold-dependent and less interpretable; (c) use a shrinkage/ridge estimator and drop the hard count entirely — trade-off: the "model discovers the number of factors" narrative disappears, but the user's deep-learning decision is untouched, since the VAE still produces the representation.

## Verdict

Steelman first: the design is not naive. It correctly refuses to fix K a priori, it notices that PCA components never stop accumulating variance, it pairs the pruning claim with a downstream estimability guard, and it explicitly acknowledges rotational indeterminacy and handles it for stability measurement (`DVT_strategie_facteurs_risque_latents_v4_1.md:908`) and for risk reporting (`:692`). The rebuttal is that the central claim nevertheless fails on three independent grounds: the count is a coordinate statistic on a basis the model does not identify, so the same fitted model yields different counts under a rotation (CRI-01); the guard the document itself calls binding makes the count a function of the sample length rather than of the data (CRI-02); and the selection metric is dimensionless in AU while the only penalty that could discipline it is inactive by construction, with the selection performed on the same OOS period used for reporting (CRI-03). The supporting machinery adds arithmetic errors (MAJ-04), a capacity ratio that counts overlapping windows it declares redundant (MAJ-05), a PCA comparison confounded by a 6-17× factor-count difference (MAJ-06), and a characterization dashboard that contradicts the DVT's own invariance result (MAJ-08). What survives is the legitimate part of the intent — K as a representation capacity — but it is being asked to answer a question it cannot answer. The deep-learning path that a linear model cannot replicate is not "more factors"; it is a *conditional* risk object (regime/tail-dependent covariance, or a density) whose complexity is selected by an OOS forecast loss, with the factor count defined on a rotation-invariant functional. Score: 3/10.
