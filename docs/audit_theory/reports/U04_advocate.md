## Five questions

**1. Purpose and original intent.** The unit converts raw price histories into a stationary, scale-free `(T,2)` tensor — log returns (DVT:208), 21-day realized volatility (DVT:244-246), per-window and per-feature z-scoring (DVT:226-248), T = 504 (DVT:295-301) — and labels stress windows for loss reweighting (DVT:544-558). The stated intent is explicit: normalization "forces the model to learn *structural patterns* ... rather than absolute return levels — which is precisely what a risk factor model requires" (DVT:240), while the volatility channel restores the dynamics that standardization removes (DVT:250). T is declared an "architectural constraint, not a choice about how much history to consider" (DVT:297), because memory is carried by aggregation over all windows (DVT:656). The ISD restates the same contract as CONV-01/02/10 and INV-005 (ISD:877-881). The design therefore separates three objects: *shape* (encoder input), *scale* (σ_i,t/σ̄_t rescaling, DVT:728), and *stress* (γ weights, DVT:588).

**2. What a state-of-the-art version would look like.** (i) Per-instance normalization with an explicit reversible scale path, as in RevIN (Kim, Kim, Tae, Park, Choi & Choo, 2022, "Reversible Instance Normalization for Accurate Time-Series Forecasting against Distribution Shift", ICLR) — the corpus implements the estimator manually rather than learning the de-normalization [unverified]. (ii) A more efficient volatility measurement than a 21-day close-to-close std: range-based estimators (Parkinson, 1980, "The Extreme Value Method for Estimating the Variance of the Rate of Return", *Journal of Business*) or high-frequency realized measures (Andersen, Bollerslev, Diebold & Labys, 2003, "Modeling and Forecasting Realized Volatility", *Econometrica*; Barndorff-Nielsen & Shephard, 2002, "Econometric Analysis of Realized Volatility...", *JRSS-B*) [unverified]. (iii) State-dependent exposures rather than a single scale-free B, as in conditional autoencoders (Gu, Kelly & Xiu, 2021, "Autoencoder Asset Pricing Models", *Journal of Econometrics*) [unverified], motivated by asymmetric/extreme correlations (Longin & Solnik, 2001, *Journal of Finance*; Ang & Chen, 2002, *Journal of Financial Economics*) [unverified]. (iv) Feature ablations that separate "richer data" from "more complex model" — which Benchmark 2.6 does for PCA (Benchmark:150-158) but not for the VAE.

**3. What prevents the unit from reaching it — apparent vs real obstacles.** *Apparent:* "z-scoring destroys volatility level" — false as a design criticism; scale is relocated by construction to the rescaling (ADV-01, ADV-11); "VIX labels leak" — false; the label is training-only and excluded from the validation ELBO (ADV-07); "two features are too few" — false as a hard limit; F is modular at ~0.1% parameter cost (ADV-04, DVT:256); "T = 504 is arbitrary" — partly false; it is tied to the minimum listing history and the scale hierarchy (ADV-05). *Real:* the scale-invariance assumption (ADV-11); the volatility channel is a transform, not new information (ADV-04); the P80 label measures regime, not tail (ADV-06); the 21-day input horizon and the 252-day rescaling horizon are mismatched (DVT:734, ADV-10); T and the vol windows are marked "resolved" without a sensitivity protocol (DVT:998, DVT:1016 — ADV-05); and the benchmark document understates the warm-up (ADV-10).

Move the >5-day gap rule from whole-window exclusion to a gap mask/weight, preserving kernel-scale continuity without deleting up to 504 windows (ADV-08). Remove the "T resolved" status; keep the scale hierarchy, not the number 504 (ADV-05). Fold the separate 21-day warm-up into the volatility component's warm-up (ADV-10). Keep the VIX label and the γ weighting separate from the volatility feature; their apparent duplication is functional (input vs loss weight, DVT:580).

## Core strengths to preserve

1. **Exact shape/scale separation** (ADV-01): the only reason a mean over calm and crisis windows is coherent at all. Any redesign must keep a scale-free representation *and* an explicit, separately estimated scale path.
2. **Point-in-time construction of every feature statistic** (ADV-02, ADV-10): per-window μ,σ, 21-day pre-window vol, 252-day trailing vol, expanding-training VIX threshold. This is a non-negotiable invariant.
3. **Per-feature independent normalization** (ADV-03): prevents the volatility channel from dominating the return channel; joint normalization would silently destroy the design.
4. **Architecture-consistent scale hierarchy** (ADV-05): 5/21/63/252/504 days, each tied to a kernel or a modeling role. The discipline matters more than the specific numbers.
5. **Training-only, validation-excluded crisis weighting** (ADV-07): the cleanest leakage control in the corpus; preserve the separation between gradient reweighting and model selection.
6. **The η algebra** (ADV-06): a reusable, correct diagnostic for any reweighting scheme, including future ones.
7. **Modular feature dimension** (ADV-04, DVT:256, DVT:303): feature experiments cost ~0.1% of parameters; this is the cheapest route to testing ADV-04 and ADV-11.

## Verdict

The feature/preprocessing unit is the most rigorously constructed part of the theory. Its core mathematics is exact, its look-ahead discipline is explicit and verifiable, and the shape/scale split is not an ad hoc normalization but the mechanism that makes the anti-cyclical aggregation meaningful. The weaknesses are semantic and evidential rather than mechanical: two unrelated volatility horizons share one name, the volatility channel's "non-redundancy" is overclaimed, the P80 stress label measures regime rather than tail, the scale-invariance of exposures is assumed rather than tested, and there are unit and warm-up slips in the delisting and benchmark documents. None of these requires abandoning the design; they require stating the conditions and testing the assumptions. As advocate, I would keep the pipeline and change the claims, not the other way round.

Score: 7/10
