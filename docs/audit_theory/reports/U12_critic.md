## Five questions

**1. Purpose and original intent.** The unit exists to stop a pure risk-minimization objective from collapsing into the minimum-variance corner: "risk minimization is the primary goal, factorial diversification is the means" (`DVT_strategie_facteurs_risque_latents_v4_1.md:765`). The instrument is the Shannon entropy of *principal-factor* risk contributions, adopted because raw contributions "can be **negative** when Σ_z has non-zero off-diagonal terms" (`DVT_strategie_facteurs_risque_latents_v4_1.md:684`), with the subsidiary claim that "The rotation ... resolves the rotational indeterminacy of the VAE latent space" (`DVT_strategie_facteurs_risque_latents_v4_1.md:692`). The metric is meant to serve three roles at once: objective term, diagnostic (ENB — `literature_review_idiosyncratic_risk.md:197`), and hypothesis-selection score (`ISD_vae_latent_risk_factors.md:2261-2263`).

**2. State of the art.** A defensible version separates the three roles. (i) Diversification as a *constraint*, not a reward: risk budgeting with existence/uniqueness results for equal risk contributions (Maillard, Roncalli & Teïletche 2010, "The Properties of Equally Weighted Risk Contribution Portfolios", *JPM* 36(4) — ERC exists and is unique when contributions are positive; with uncorrelated assets ERC weights ∝ 1/σ) and its convex log-barrier solution (Spinu 2013, "An Algorithm for Computing Risk Parity Weights", SSRN 2297383) — both [unverified], cited by the corpus at `Benchmark_latent_risk_factor.md:333-337`. (ii) Diversification measures that are *invariant to the factor partition*: Meucci 2009, "Managing Diversification", *Risk* 22(5), shows ENB = exp(H) of risk contributions and that ENB depends on the chosen set of "bets"; the corpus cites Meucci but adopts the measure without the invariance caveat (`DVT_strategie_facteurs_risque_latents_v4_1.md:684,706`) [unverified]. (iii) A covariance estimator whose spectral loss matches the spectral use: nonlinear shrinkage (Ledoit & Wolf 2012, "Nonlinear shrinkage estimation of large-dimensional covariance matrices", *Annals of Statistics*) instead of linear shrinkage toward a scaled identity [unverified]. (iv) Explicit treatment of eigen-direction unreliability (Davis & Kahan 1970, "The rotation of eigenvectors by a perturbation. III", *SIAM J. Numer. Anal.*: the subspace angle is bounded by ‖E‖/gap, so clustered eigenvalues leave directions unidentified; Johnstone 2001, "On the distribution of the largest eigenvalue in PCA", *Annals of Statistics*: sample eigenvalues form a Marchenko–Pastur bulk and small ones are indistinguishable from noise) [unverified]. (v) For a deep-learning formulation that a linear model cannot match: a *conditional* risk model — the encoder emits a predictive distribution over factor covariances (mixture/flow over the latent), and the allocator minimizes a risk functional under that mixture (e.g. E_p[w'Σw] + κ·CVaR of the predictive portfolio return), which PCA cannot produce because it has one unconditional covariance.

**3. What prevents it.** Findings U12-CRI-01…10. The strongest defense of the current design, steelmanned: equal risk contributions is the only symmetric, scale-free, return-free allocation rule, and the principal-factor rotation is the minimal orthogonal device that makes the contributions non-negative while leaving the asset covariance unchanged (`DVT_strategie_facteurs_risque_latents_v4_1.md:692`); entropy is the only concentration measure that is additive over independent bets, so ENB is canonical. Rebuttal: every one of those virtues is stated relative to the *estimated* decomposition, and the estimation step destroys the independent-bets interpretation (CRI-02, -03, -09); the rotation solves the sign problem, not the degeneracy or the partition problem.

**4. Real constraints vs self-imposed choices.** Real: long-only/simplex/cap constraints; negative contributions in a non-diagonal basis; factor covariances are estimated; the user's DL constraint; non-CRSP data limits history length and hence AU (and therefore the eigen-tail). Self-imposed: entropy (rather than any other concentration statistic) of *risk contributions*, uniform budgets, the *principal-factor* basis as the allocation space, the scalarized sum with an α "elbow", the 0.20 idiosyncratic mixing weight, making Ĥ the primary evaluation metric, and keeping a symmetric variance functional after the corpus repeatedly asserts that episodic/tail risk is the object of interest.

**5. What should be removed, merged or moved.** Remove: the entropy *reward* as a scalarized term (and with it α, the α/λ grid and the "elbow", `DVT_strategie_facteurs_risque_latents_v4_1.md:814`) — replace by a diversification *constraint* (min w'Σw s.t. max_k ĉ'_k ≤ c, or s.t. ENB ≥ B), which restores the declared hierarchy and removes the non-convexity admitted at L818. Remove: Ĥ_OOS as primary metric (`Benchmark_latent_risk_factor.md:172`); move diversification measurement onto realized OOS factor returns. Merge: the factor and idiosyncratic layers into a single asset-space dispersion statistic, deleting the 0.20 weight (`literature_review_idiosyncratic_risk.md:236-301`). Merge: Strategy A and Strategy B into one risk-budgeting framework where b is a declared input defaulting to uniform (`strategy_comparison.md:32`), so the return-based budget becomes an explicit directional overlay consistent with `strategy_philosophy.md:156-161`. Move: the episodic/tail concern out of the VAE training loss and the diagnostics and into the risk functional (predictive mixture or ES), where a deep conditional model can act on it. Move: the principal-factor rotation out of the objective and into the risk model, where it is only a diagnostic.

## Root-cause hypotheses

**RC-1 — The diversification metric is defined on the estimated covariance, not on the risk the investor bears.** Symptoms: CRI-01, -02, -03, -04, -10. Every property claimed for the objective ("equal", "no dominant concentration", "well-defined") is a property of Σ̂, and the corpus never asks whether it survives the estimator, the regime mixture, or the basis choice. Structural alternatives: (a) define diversification as a constraint that must hold for every covariance in an ambiguity set around Σ̂ (distributionally robust risk budgeting) — trade-off: more conservative, higher ex-ante variance, but the property is guaranteed rather than asserted; (b) allocate on a *posterior* over contributions, maximizing the probability of meeting a concentration bound rather than a point estimate — trade-off: needs a prior and destroys the "one number" diagnostic; (c) collapse the eigen-tail and allocate over a small, well-separated, economically identified factor set — trade-off: reintroduces a priori structure and weakens the "let the data reveal factors" claim.

**RC-2 — One scalar is made to carry three unrelated functions: risk control, diversification, and model/hyperparameter selection.** Symptoms: CRI-04, -05, -09, -11. Because the same H appears as reward, as diagnostic, and as primary OOS score, the strategy selects for the quantity it optimizes. Structural alternatives: (a) hard separation — risk minimization as objective, diversification as constraint, selection on realized OOS risk contributions; (b) a single risk functional that already encodes the diversification preference (expected shortfall with a concentration penalty); (c) lexicographic/staged optimisation with a declared order. Trade-offs: (a) requires a defensible constraint level; (b) loses the interpretable ENB; (c) reintroduces the calibration problem in another form.

**RC-3 — The factor partition is treated as neutral while every object used downstream (λ_k, V, AU, ĉ) is estimated and enters the objective non-linearly.** Symptoms: CRI-02, -03, -09, -12. Structural alternatives: (a) spectral-loss-aligned estimation (nonlinear shrinkage) so the allocation consumes a spectrum corrected for the use it is put to; (b) allocate in an estimation-stable space (clusters/hierarchy rather than eigen-directions) — trade-off: loses orthogonality and the clean gradient; (c) integrate parameter uncertainty into the allocation itself. Trade-offs: (a) does not fix basis non-identification, only eigenvalue bias; (b) makes the objective non-smooth and undermines the SCA machinery; (c) is expensive and makes the risk decomposition a distribution, not a vector.

**RC-4 — The proposal layer is never reconciled with the specification layer, so "the objective" has three incompatible definitions.** Symptoms: CRI-06, -07, -08, -11. The DVT has H over factors; the idiosyncratic review adds a two-layer H with weight 0.20; the comparison document adds Sortino budgets; the benchmark asserts that PCA uses "the same objective". Any of the three proposals, if adopted, breaks the fairness condition (INV-012, `ISD_vae_latent_risk_factors.md:1960`). Structural alternative: make the objective an *interface with mandatory invariants* — sign convention, basis invariance, partition invariance, comparability across AU — that any proposal must satisfy and that the benchmark must share; keep proposals in a pre-registered experiment register rather than in the theory text. Trade-off: less room for narrative synthesis, more discipline.

**RC-5 — Asymmetry is acknowledged everywhere and admitted nowhere in the risk functional.** Symptoms: CRI-01, -08. The episodic-risk principle, the VIX crisis weighting, and the crisis-return metric all point at the tail, but the objective is a symmetric quadratic in a covariance estimated on a regime mixture. Structural alternatives: (a) a conditional/mixture covariance as the risk functional (the encoder predicts a distribution over Σ, the allocator minimizes the mixture risk) — this is the deep-learning formulation a linear PCA model cannot match, because PCA has one unconditional covariance and cannot make the risk matrix state-dependent; (b) an explicit downside decomposition (semi-covariance or ES contributions) as the allocation object — trade-off: half the effective sample, non-PSD, loses the clean eigen-rotation; (c) retain the symmetric objective but change the learned object to a predictive return distribution so the optimizer can price the tail. Testable prediction for (a): the conditional covariance must beat the unconditional one on OOS realized-variance/ES forecast error (QLIKE or joint loss) by a margin exceeding the bootstrap interval; if it does not, the conditional formulation adds nothing and the symmetric objective is vindicated.

## Verdict

The algebra in this unit is sound — I re-derived the gradient and it checks out (`DVT_strategie_facteurs_risque_latents_v4_1.md:702`) — but the algebra is the least load-bearing part. The unit's central claim, that equalizing principal-factor risk contributions means "no dominant risk concentration", fails three independent tests: it equalizes the wrong moment (average, not tail — CRI-01), it equalizes a biased spectrum (shrinkage inverts the target — CRI-02), and its contributions are not identifiable when eigenvalues cluster (CRI-03). On top of that, the number used to decide whether the whole theory works is the objective function itself, evaluated on the model that was optimized to maximize it, and it is scale-invariant so it cannot even distinguish risk levels (CRI-04).
