## Five questions

**1. Purpose and original intent (cite the spec).** §4.7 constructs a *risk-only* portfolio layer on top of the VAE risk model: maximize Shannon entropy of **principal-factor** risk contributions (684–692) subject to long-only, budget, 5 % hard cap, 3 % soft concentration threshold with φ₀≈25, semi-continuous w_min=0.10 %, and a 30 % one-way turnover cap (790–812); the entropy weight α is calibrated at the **elbow of the variance–entropy frontier** at fixed λ=1.0 (814); the non-concave problem is solved by multi-start SCA with Armijo backtracking (820–844), cardinality by one-at-a-time entropy-aware elimination (834–842), and update frequencies are monthly-to-quarterly retraining with portfolio re-optimisation (848–858). MOD-008 restates the same contract and adds test obligations (1823–1980).

**2. What a state-of-the-art version would look like.** The spec's own framing is already dated. Factor *risk budgeting* with equal or custom budgets is a **convex** problem when written in log-space or as a fixed-point in risk contributions (Spinu 2013; Roncalli 2013, *Introduction to Risk Parity and Budgeting*, ch. 6–7; Maillard, Roncalli & Teïletche 2010). A modern implementation would (i) define a homogeneous risk measure and solve the risk-budgeting equations with a provably convergent fixed point, rather than a non-convex entropy surrogate; (ii) if entropy itself is the objective, work in **variance-normalised / correlation space** so that the Hessian scale is O(1), which removes the need for gradient clipping and α renormalisation (Boyd & Vandenberghe, *Convex Optimization*, ch. 9–11); (iii) handle cardinality as an explicit MIQP or a separate universe-selection stage (Bertsimas & Cory-Wright 2022; Lobo, Fazel & Boyd 2007), not as a post-hoc greedy patched onto a continuous solution; (iv) calibrate diversification against a **mandate-level target** fixed ex ante (e.g. an ENB or ex-ante TE budget) rather than re-deriving a heuristic on the same data used to score the strategy; (v) enforce costs with a single cost model that is *the same* model used to evaluate net returns (Almgren & Chriss 2000 is an execution-scheduling result, not a one-period penalty).

**3. What prevents the current unit from reaching it (evidenced).** The objective that is *calibrated* is not the objective that is *solved*, which is not the objective that is *measured*: factor-only entropy at the spec level (684–692), a 95/5 factor/idiosyncratic mixture in the optimizer (`entropy.py:94`), a tilted budget in the notebook path, and factor-only entropy again for the ENB target and for the reported Ĥ (`pipeline.py:2645–2653`). On top of that, α is rescaled by a data-dependent factor (`sca_solver.py:634`) and then the rescaled value is **not** forwarded to the cardinality stage (`pipeline.py:2666–2682`). The solver's own output shows it does not maximise its objective (non-monotone frontier, `diagnostic_data.json:205`).

**4. Real vs self-imposed constraints.** Real: long-only, Σw=1, position caps (mandate/liquidity), transaction costs, and the finite AU. Self-imposed: the *non-convex entropy objective* (a risk-budget would be convex); the Lagrangian α as the diversification dial; the 0.7/floor-2 ENB heuristic; gradient clipping at 10; α renormalisation at equal weight; multi-start at all (a convex problem has one solution); the semi-continuous w_min handled inside the optimiser instead of by universe pre-selection; the Kneedle/elbow machinery (dead code by default); and the 5-start × 100-iteration SCA budget.

**5. What should be removed, merged or moved.** Remove: the α grid and the entire frontier/elbow/Kneedle calibration (791–814, `frontier.py`), multi-start (`sca_solver.py:1059–1150`), the fast PGD subproblem (`sca_solver.py:245–350`), the two-layer idiosyncratic entropy and the tilted budget (pick one definition of "risk contribution"), the momentum module (`momentum.py`, dormant but wired at `pipeline.py:2492–2501`), and the batched cardinality heuristics (keep exactly one method). Merge: all hard constraints into one exact projection/DCP model; one cost model shared by optimiser and evaluation. Move: active-set/cardinality selection out of the optimiser into a separate, auditable universe-selection stage; the diversification target from a runtime heuristic to an ex-ante mandate parameter.

## Root-cause hypotheses

**R1 — The objective is under-specified in units, and every symptom is a patch on that.** Factor entropy is scale-invariant and bounded by ln(AU); variance is in (daily) return² and unbounded in relative terms; idiosyncratic risk is ~half of total risk but has no principled place in the metric. Consequently α/λ has no stable meaning, and the code responds with four independent reparameterisations: α renormalisation (`sca_solver.py:634`), gradient clipping (`entropy.py:202`), a second entropy layer (`entropy.py:94`), and a tilted budget (`entropy.py:88–120`). Each changes the optimisation problem rather than the formulation; none is in the spec; together they explain CRI-01/03, MAJ-07/09/10.
Alternatives: (a) *Work in standardised space.* Divide each asset return by its vol and each factor by √λ; the objective becomes O(1) by construction, α regains a unit meaning, and clipping/normalisation disappear. Trade-off: destroys the level information that the volatility rescaling in §4.7 was designed to preserve. (b) *Replace the Lagrangian by a constraint.* Minimise wᵀΣw s.t. ENB(w) ≥ target with the target fixed ex ante by mandate. Trade-off: one fewer free parameter, but ENB is non-convex, so the constraint buys a hard feasibility problem. (c) *Replace entropy by risk budgeting.* Specify a budget vector b on total risk contributions (systematic + idiosyncratic) and solve the risk-budgeting equations. Trade-off: convex and provable, but equal *risk contributions* is not equal *entropy*; the "diversification" semantics change and must be re-argued.

**R2 — The solver is a black box whose failures are managed downstream instead of measured.** Because the subproblem, the Armijo test and the convergence flag are all approximate (MAJ-01..04, 11, 14), the artefacts show a non-optimal frontier (CRI-07) and a boundary α (CRI-02). Rather than certify the solution, the pipeline adds guards: force α>0, clamp Δ_surr, clip gradients, accept 2/5 starts converging (`strategic_paths_post_v2.md:83`). This is a *process* root cause: there is no optimality residual recorded per rebalance, so a broken solve is indistinguishable from a good one in `results/`.
Alternatives: (a) *Certified convex reformulation.* Solve the risk-budget fixed point in log-space, where convergence is monotone and the residual is a certificate. Trade-off: no longer literally maximises entropy. (b) *Two-level split with a guaranteed projection.* Optimise only in factor space (AU≈12–26) where the problem is small enough to solve globally, then project to assets with a convex QP. Trade-off: projection error, and the asset-level constraints may make the factor target infeasible. (c) *Keep SCA but instrument it.* Make the KKT residual a first-class output and refuse to trade when it exceeds a threshold. Trade-off: exposes how often the strategy cannot be traded, which is the honest outcome.

**R3 — Constraint semantics live in three places and no single source of truth.** Constraints appear as (i) objective penalties (φ, κ₁, κ₂), (ii) hard constraints in one solver path only (τ_max, w_max in CVXPY), and (iii) post-hoc projections (cardinality, clip-renormalise). Each path enforces a different subset; the default fast path enforces the smallest subset. This single cause generates CRI-05/06, MAJ-06/12/13 and the unenforced turnover cap.
Alternatives: (a) *One exact projection operator.* Maintain feasibility by construction at every iterate, with the L0 handled by a fixed universe set chosen outside the optimiser. Trade-off: the active set becomes static between selections. (b) *One DCP model for everything*, with cardinality as MIQP; drop the PGD path. Trade-off: 10–50× slower, and MIQP is not certifiably optimal at n=800. (c) *Two-stage architecture*: a separate, auditable eligibility/selection stage from which a convex constrained QP is solved on a small active set. Trade-off: selection risk replaces optimiser risk, and the selection rule itself must be validated.

**R4 — Calibration is done on the object being judged, with free constants standing in for economic priors.** 0.7, floor 2.0, 0.90, patience 2, max_grad_norm 10, MIN_BATCH_SIZE 3, _MAX_BINARY_VARS 150, φ=0, idio_weight 0.05/0.20 are all unvalidated and jointly determine the operating point. Because the walk-forward selects configs on OOS score (DVT:931–935), these constants are effectively fitted to the evaluation period, which is why "the strategy is calibrated" is not a falsifiable statement (MAJ-07/08, MIN-02, CRI-02).
Alternatives: (a) *Fix the target from the mandate* (e.g. ex-ante vol budget and a minimum ENB), with no calibration at all. Trade-off: may be infeasible on the actual risk model. (b) *Calibrate on a pre-holdout period with an explicit loss* (realised vs target variance over the next quarter) and freeze, reporting the calibration sample. Trade-off: needs a second holdout, costs data. (c) *Eliminate the dial* by using risk budgeting with a budget vector fixed by mandate. Trade-off: loses the variance/diversification trade-off as a free dimension.

**R5 — "Refresh the risk model, keep the decision" — the pipeline separates the state from the policy.** `refresh_risk_model_oos=True` changes Σ, D_ε, B' at every rebalance while α and the calibration are frozen (MAJ-15, CRI-04). Economically this is a policy that is only valid on the day it was fitted; statistically it makes the OOS path a different strategy from the one whose α was selected. It also breaks the like-for-like comparison with benchmarks, which re-fit and re-solve.
Alternatives: (a) Freeze the risk model for the whole OOS window (as the benchmarks originally did) so the comparison isolates the model. Trade-off: stale risk model. (b) Re-solve the operating point at each rebalance on the refreshed model. Trade-off: cost and a new source of path dependence. (c) Express the policy in a scale-free statistic (target ENB or target tracking error) that is re-solved each rebalance, so the *decision rule* is invariant to the risk-model refresh. Trade-off: requires a root-find per rebalance.

## Verdict

The unit is the weakest link in the architecture and it fails in a way that is invisible from the documents. The mathematics that *is* present is often correct — the principal-basis entropy gradient (DVT:696–704) is derived correctly and implemented correctly (`entropy.py:143–195`), and the simplex/box projection is sound — but it is applied to an objective that is no longer the specified one (a 95/5 factor/idiosyncratic mixture, optionally tilted), with a scale reparameterisation that voids the α calibration, an inner solver that solves half the risk gradient with an invalid step size and no turnover constraint, a cardinality stage that re-optimises a different α than the one calibrated, and a set of hard constraints that are neither enforced on the default solver path nor checked anywhere. The only real-data artefact confirms the theory is not operative: α lands on the grid boundary, the entropy curve is non-monotone and saturates, and the achieved diversification is ENB≈2.7 of 12 factors — 0.40 normalised, against a documented "typical" 0.80–0.95 — while the target that defines success was itself set by an unvalidated 0.7× heuristic on a mislabelled quantity. The most damaging structural fact is not any single bug: it is that the strategy's diversification claim is *measured* on factor-only entropy, *optimised* on a mixture, *calibrated* on a heuristic, and *rebalanced* on a fourth functional, and no artefact records whether the solver ever converged. This is not a unit that needs patches; it needs its objective re-derived (one risk measure, one budget, one constraint model) before any of its numbers can be believed.

Score: 2/10
