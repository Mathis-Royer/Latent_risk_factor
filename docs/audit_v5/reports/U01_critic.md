## Five questions

**1. Purpose and original intent (cite the spec).** The unit states the *raison d'être*: "The goal is not to maximize returns through a prediction algorithm, but to **minimize risk through optimal diversification**" (`docs/DVT_…v4_1.md:25`); "identify the truly independent sources of risk, then build a portfolio where no exposure is unintentionally duplicated" (`docs/strategy_philosophy.md:9`). It asserts an epistemic asymmetry — "Return prediction requires the model to be *right about the future*… Risk identification… requires the model to be *right about the present structure*" (`DVT:27–29`) — and derives four design commitments: continuous multi-membership exposures (§2.2–2.3), memory of dormant risks (§2.3, philosophy §4), no *a priori* categories (§1.3, philosophy §2), and a risk object defined as "the effective correlation of assets, regardless of its origin" (§2.4, `DVT:89`).

**2. What a state-of-the-art version would look like.** A defensible "risk-first, no-return-forecast" unit would (i) define risk as a *conditional predictive loss distribution* over the holding horizon, not as an unconditional second moment — since a portfolio's risk is by definition forward-looking (Meucci 2009 [39] and Roncalli–Weisang 2012 [37] are already in the bibliography); (ii) treat latent factors as a *shrinkage device for the covariance*, judged by out-of-sample covariance loss (Frobenius/Stein), not by Sharpe — the standard statistical-factor literature it does not cite: Connor & Korajczyk (1986/1988) asymptotic PCA, Bai & Ng (2002, cited as [27] only as a sanity check); (iii) for price-based nonlinear factors, benchmark against Gu–Kelly–Xiu (2021, [11]) / Kelly–Pruitt–Su characteristic-based covariance, and take the "complexity must earn its place" test as a covariance-forecast test; (iv) if the motivating risk is *episodic/tail*, use a tail functional (CVaR/ES) or a t-copula factor model rather than Gaussian variance — because covariance is structurally blind to tail dependence; (v) if the claim is "diversified across all factors," measure realized risk-contribution dispersion out-of-sample (Meucci's ENB on *realized* contributions), not in-sample.

**3. What prevents the current unit from reaching it (evidenced).** Five concrete blockers, all evidenced below: (a) per-window z-scoring deletes risk magnitude before the model ever sees it (`windowing.py:251–260`); (b) the only temporal machinery in force is two EWMA half-lives set to *short* values in the one real run (aggregation 60 windows at stride 1 ≈ 3 months; Σ_z 252 days) — contradicting the "permanent memory" premise (`results/diagnostic/config.json`; `composite.py:133–144`; `pipeline.py:1976–1996`); (c) the opposite of "no priori bias" is hard-coded as an all-ones market column (`config.py:439–448`), and the latent geometry is supervised toward a Spearman correlation target (`loss.py:494–515`); (d) the risk functional is quadratic, so the stated tail/episodic risk is not representable (`entropy.py:151`; `covariance.py:6`); (e) the evaluation is Sharpe-based (`philosophy.md:183`; `results/diagnostic/report.txt:6`) while the theory is risk-based, so the unit is never actually tested.

**4. Real vs self-imposed constraints.**
*Real:* daily equity data with ~788 usable names × 31y; long-only, fully invested; the equity risk premium is not diversifiable in a long-only book (so a long-only portfolio necessarily has a dominant market factor); covariance must be estimated from a single realised path; compute and 16 GB.
*Self-imposed:* μ = 0 as doctrine rather than a choice (`philosophy.md:13`); the categorical exclusion of return views, which the notebook enforces by *disabling* a documented signal with the comment "violates Principle 1" (`notebooks/dashboard.ipynb` cell 11, `MOMENTUM_ENABLED = False`); the market factor's exclusion from the entropy objective (`pipeline.py:2292–2301`); per-window z-scoring; the 2-feature input; expanding-window-only (rejecting rolling estimators on doctrinal grounds, `DVT:872,1039`); Sharpe as the decision metric; and "interpretation optional *a posteriori*" (`philosophy.md:23`), which leaves the risk being diversified unidentified.

**5. What should be removed, merged or moved.**
- **Remove** the "anti-cyclical by construction / memory of dormant risks" claim (§4, `DVT:78`, Exec. Summary `DVT:9`). Nothing in the code implements it; what exists is a short-memory average, which is its opposite.
- **Remove** "no *a priori* bias" (`DVT:17,37,140`; `philosophy.md:17`) and replace it with an explicit prior register: market intercept, correlation-target loss, feature set, window length, z-scoring.
- **Remove** the three-way Structural/Style/Episodic taxonomy from the *design* narrative; it is diagnostic-only and uses day units where the prose uses months (§2.3 vs `factor_quality.py:258–284`).
- **Merge or delete** the second (realized-vol) channel: after per-window z-scoring it carries shape only; its scale content is re-injected by a single 252-day trailing-vol scalar (`rescaling.py:5`). Either model scale explicitly or drop the channel.
- **Merge** the competing definitions of "the entropy" (two bases coexist for the same portfolio: 2.304 nats on 12 signal dims vs 0.992 nats on 27 dims) and remove `H_norm_signal` as reported.
- **Move** the primary success criterion from Sharpe to an out-of-sample *risk* metric (realized variance ratio, realized ENB, stress drawdown). The "terminal principle" (`philosophy.md:183`) currently tests a claim the theory does not make.
- **Remove** "diversified across all of them — including the market" (`DVT:9`); restate as "residual-factor parity conditional on full market beta."

## Findings

**Steelman, then rebuttal.** *Steelman:* (i) A factor risk model rather than a return model is the best-documented way to get well-conditioned covariance estimates, and the shrinkage pipeline (DGJ spiked shrinkage, WLS cross-sectional regression, per-factor variance targeting) is genuinely state-of-the-art in its plumbing. (ii) The one real run is not a disaster: the VAE portfolio achieves 16.4% vol vs 19.7% for 1/N and 14.3% for min-variance, with drawdown 35.3% vs 37.8%, so the risk-control promise is partially delivered. (iii) Even after removing the market's 81.1% share, the 12 signal factors are fairly equalized (ENB ≈ 9.2 of 12, computed from `diagnostic_data.json`) — the entropy machinery does what it says *on the residual*. (iv) "Diversify, don't predict" is a legitimate and historically productive posture (DeMiguel et al. [44] is cited precisely because estimation error eats complex optimizers). *Rebuttal:* none of this rescues the unit's *distinctive* premises. The equalization is achieved by excluding the dominant risk from the objective; the risk model is miscalibrated by 2.7× on the only real data; and the "memory"/"no-prior"/"graph"/"tail" claims — the four things that make this strategy different from plain PCA-RP — are respectively contradicted by configuration, contradicted by code, unimplemented, or mathematically unrepresentable. What survives is a competently engineered statistical factor model with a market intercept; the vision is decoration around it.

## Root-cause hypotheses

**RC1 — Time is collapsed into a static snapshot, and every temporal claim is then implemented by a half-life chosen for a different purpose.** The architecture reduces a 31-year dependence process to one static B and one static Σ_z, then applies a static risk-parity rule. Because there is no state, "dormancy," "reactivation," "regime" and "memory" can only be represented by EWMA weights — and those weights were set to 3 months (composite) and 1 year (Σ_z) in the only real run, while the prose asserts permanence. Symptoms across units: CRI-03, CRI-04, CRI-16, A1–A3, and likely the variance-ratio miscalibration (U09). *Structural alternatives:* (a) **regime-conditional risk model** — a small number of covariance states with mixing weights, diversifying over the *mixture* rather than the unconditional average; trade-off: regime-inference error and label switching, more parameters; (b) **stress-scenario parity** — equalize risk contributions under a small set of historical crisis windows rather than under the stationary covariance; trade-off: conservative, may be infeasible under long-only + w_max; (c) **rolling multi-horizon ensemble** — average *portfolios* optimized under several covariance horizons instead of averaging covariances; trade-off: still no state, but honest about the ambiguity and cheap.

**RC2 — The risk functional is elliptical while the motivating risk is tail/episodic.** Every downstream artefact (Gaussian likelihood, quadratic risk, covariance factors) is second-moment. Symptoms: CRI-06, CRI-01, CRI-12, A10, and the strategy's inability to explain its own drawdowns. *Alternatives:* (a) replace variance with a coherent tail functional (ES/CVaR) computed on a factor model with tail dependence (t-copula or explicit tail factors), optimizing ES contributions; trade-off: non-convex, no closed form, harder to estimate but directly aligned with the claim; (b) keep Gaussian factors and add separately estimated **downside/tail factors** (downside beta, co-drawdown loadings) as extra columns; trade-off: partially redundant, but cheap and testable; (c) **narrow the claim**: state the objective as "diversify Gaussian factor variance" and delete the tail/episodic rationale entirely. The current document tries to keep the ambition and the tractability.

**RC3 — Normalisation destroys the quantity being modelled.** Per-window z-scoring makes every window statistically identical in scale, so the model can only learn *shape*; all scale information is re-injected as a single trailing-vol ratio. Symptoms: CRI-02, CRI-01 (λ estimated from shape-driven z-scores), CRI-11, and the low explanatory power (EP 0.36) — a shape-only factor model cannot explain variance levels. *Alternatives:* (a) feed globally standardized (not per-window) returns/volatility so scale is learnable; trade-off: non-stationarity enters the encoder, needs explicit scaling model; (b) **two-head factorization**: shape factors from z-scored windows + a separate volatility-state model, combined explicitly; trade-off: more moving parts but each estimable; (c) drop the volatility channel and declare the model a pure shape/correlation model, with scale handled entirely by the (documented) trailing-vol layer.

**RC4 — The "no prior" doctrine drives priors underground.** Because the design forbids named factors philosophically, the necessary priors are injected silently in code (all-ones market column, Spearman target, feature weights, window length, z-scoring), and their effects are never measured or reported. Symptoms: CRI-05, CRI-10, CRI-13, A4–A6, and the impossibility of attributing performance to "discovery" vs. "prior." *Alternatives:* (a) a **prior register**: every injected structure documented, with an ablation measuring its contribution; (b) remove the market intercept and estimate the market factor from returns (or keep it and rename the strategy "market-beta + residual-factor parity"); (c) if the correlation-target loss is retained, benchmark the VAE against the correlation matrix's own eigendecomposition — which is exactly what the PCA benchmarks do, making the comparison an honest test of the *nonlinearity*, not of "discovery."

**RC5 — The evaluation criterion is orthogonal to the theory.** The unit is a risk-diversification theory; its decision rule is Sharpe (`philosophy.md:183`), its reported "success" metrics are incommensurable (CRI-17), and its only real run has one fold and zero statistical tests (CRI-15). Consequently no finding in this unit — true or false — can be confirmed or refuted by the existing evidence base. *Alternatives:* (a) primary criterion = out-of-sample risk-forecast accuracy (variance ratio, correlation-matrix Frobenius loss, realized ENB) with Sharpe as a secondary constraint; (b) pre-registered dominance test vs PCA-RP on a risk metric; (c) if Sharpe remains the criterion, delete the "we do not predict returns" framing, because then the project is a return-seeking strategy with an unusually elaborate covariance estimator.

## Verdict

The unit contains a real insight — a long-only equity book cannot diversify its market beta, and residual-factor risk *can* be measured and spread — but it surrounds that insight with four load-bearing claims that do not survive contact with the code or the results. The "memory of dormant risks" is, in the only real run, a three-month exponential average of z-scored shapes with a one-year covariance memory; the "no *a priori* bias" claim coexists with a hand-appended market factor and a correlation-target training loss; "risk as a graph" is a bipartite star with diagonal idiosyncratic risk; and the risk definition chosen in §2.4 is mathematically incapable of representing the tail/episodic risk that §2.3 says motivates everything. Worse, the central anti-cyclical formula (|β′| ∝ 1/√λ) is self-defeating as a protection mechanism: it maximises exposure to the factors whose variance is currently lowest, so reactivation converts an ex-ante 1/AU contribution into a dominant one — the doc presents this as insurance when it is the opposite. The one quantitative result available supports the sceptical reading rather than the vision: 81.1% of systematic risk in a single factor that the objective deliberately excludes, and a risk model that under-predicts realized variance by 2.7×. The vision should keep the residual-factor-parity core, delete the dormancy/no-prior/graph/tail claims, and re-base its success criterion on out-of-sample covariance accuracy.

Score: 3/10
