## Five questions

**1. Purpose and original intent.** The unit is the strategy's only source of risk selection once `mu=0`: "The strategy's declared objective — 'minimize risk through optimal diversification' — is a two-level hierarchy: risk minimization is the primary goal, factorial diversification is the means" (`docs/DVT_strategie_facteurs_risque_latents_v4_1.md:765`). The intended object is "the entropy of principal factor risk contributions" (same file:682-690), made well-defined by diagonalising `Sigma_z`: "The entropy is then always well-defined" (:688). The philosophical statement is stronger than the spec: "It equalizes the contribution of each factor to total portfolio risk" (`docs/strategy_philosophy.md:88`), "maximized when all contributions are equal" (:102) and "no single principal factor dominates the portfolio's risk" (:140). The spec's own operating-point language is weaker and more honest: `alpha=0` is min-variance, `alpha/lambda -> inf` is maximum entropy, "Intermediate `alpha`: ... **This is the operating point**" (`docs/DVT...:772-774`).

**2. State of the art for this objective.** The defensible SOTA is not "entropy of contributions scalarized against variance"; it is budgeted factor risk parity written as a convex problem: `min ½ w'Σw − Σ_k b_k ln(β_k)` subject to `w≥0`, which yields `RC_k = b_k σ_p` exactly (Maillard, Roncalli & Teiletche 2010 for ERC; Spinu 2013 for the convex ERC form; Roncalli & Weisang 2016 for risk parity with risk factors; both Roncalli references are already in `docs/strategy_comparison.md:172-173`). Meucci (2009) supplies ENB (=`exp(H)`) as a *diagnostic*, not an objective (`docs/DVT...:684`). If downside awareness is wanted, the literature move is ES/CVaR risk contributions, not Sortino budgets — the repo itself cites Estrada (2006) and Ang, Chen & Xing (2006) for semi-variance (`docs/strategy_comparison.md:118`). For within-factor control, the standard device is an explicit concentration bound per factor, which the repo already names as R1 (`docs/strategy_comparison.md:117`). For the α problem, the SOTA is a *constraint* (`max H s.t. Var ≤ σ²_target` or `s.t. ENB ≥ target`), not a scalarised reward with an unconstrained price.

**3. What prevents it.** Evidenced obstacles: (i) the α that actually prices diversification is overwritten by `_compute_alpha_eff`, which rescales α by the ratio of gradient norms at equal weight (`src/portfolio/sca_solver.py:592-598, 633-634`) — the doc's α grid and "α in return units" (`:1052`) therefore have no fixed meaning; (ii) the selection rule maximises nothing: it picks min variance among α that clear a target ENB whose floor is 2.0 (`src/portfolio/frontier.py:403-430, 535-537`), and when that yields α=0 a guard forces the smallest positive α (`src/integration/pipeline.py:2610-2621`); the one real run ends at `"alpha_opt": 0.001` (`results/diagnostic/diagnostic_data.json:167`), 1.2% above the α=0 variance (`:219-232`); (iii) the frontier records `H` *without* the budget the optimiser used (`src/portfolio/frontier.py:199-208, 320-327`) and the report divides a 27-bin entropy by ln(12) (`results/diagnostic/diagnostic_data.json:116, 183, 299`). Only apparent obstacles: the non-concavity of `H` (`docs/DVT...:818`) is real but avoidable — the convex log-barrier formulation reaches the same first-order condition, and the repo already implements the convex two-stage projection (`src/portfolio/cardinality.py:525`); the `n >> AU` "drowning" problem (`src/portfolio/entropy.py:12-16`) is avoidable by normalising each layer by its own maximum instead of introducing a second free weight.

**4. Real vs self-imposed constraints.** Real: long-only plus `1'w=1` makes `c'_k = const` infeasible when the eigenvalue spectrum spans decades — the run shows ENB 2.70 against max entropy `ln(27)=3.296` (`results/diagnostic/diagnostic_data.json:184, 299`); the market principal factor cannot be equalised, and the code correctly refuses to try (`src/walk_forward/oos_rebalancing.py:325-330`); ENB depends on the chosen factor partition (Meucci 2009); scalarisation reaches only supported points of a non-convex frontier, and the frontier is visibly non-monotone (`results/diagnostic/diagnostic_data.json:205-232`). Self-imposed: the entropy-vs-variance scalarisation itself, the α grid, the ENB-target elbow, the idio entropy layer, the gradient clip, and the runtime budget switch (`src/config.py:615-616`).

**5. Remove / merge / move.** Remove `_compute_alpha_eff` — it is not in either cited reference and it destroys α's units. Remove `H_idio` from the objective and move cardinality/within-factor control to one explicit constraint (HHI or effective-N per factor), which is where the philosophy already puts it (`docs/strategy_philosophy.md:142-146`). Remove the `entropy_budget_mode` switch or complete it: today the in-fold frontier can be tilted while every OOS rebalance is uniform (`src/walk_forward/oos_rebalancing.py:449-470` passes no `budget`). Merge the two H definitions into one budget-aware function used for optimisation *and* evaluation. Move the PC1 exclusion from a silent index skip to an explicit market-risk budget. If the constrained formulation is adopted, the entire frontier/Kneedle/α-grid machinery becomes a diagnostic plot, not a calibration engine — that removes the largest block of code in this unit without losing any of the mathematics.

## Core strengths to preserve

1. **The principal-factor basis, and only that basis.** `c'_k = λ_k β_k'²` is the one construction in this unit that is simultaneously exact (contributions sum to systematic variance), sign-free, immune to VAE rotational indeterminacy, and cheap (`O(n·AU)`). Any redesign that computes risk contributions in raw latent or asset space loses all four properties. Evidence: `src/portfolio/entropy.py:149-152`; `docs/DVT...:684-692`.
2. **The analytic gradient and its closed-form stationarity.** `φ_k = λ_k β_k(ln ĉ_k + H)` is correct, tested against finite differences, and vanishes exactly at equal contributions. Keep it even if the objective is reformulated — it is the reference against which any replacement (log-barrier, constrained) must be checked.
3. **`H_tilted = −KL(ĉ‖b)` with its exact reduction to standard entropy at uniform `b`.** This is a correctly implemented, tested capability that allows budgeted factor risk parity to be compared on the *same* solver and constraints — the right experimental design for U10's Sortino question. Keep the code even if the runtime path is simplified.
4. **ENB computed on the factor-only entropy with the market factor excluded.** The code's own rationale (`frontier.py:391-396`) and the 81%/ENB=2.70 evidence show why: a combined or full-AU ENB is either trivially inflated or unattainable. The reporting discipline (factor-only ENB, separate idio diagnostics) must survive the redesign even if the objective changes.
5. **The honest two-sided comparison with Sortino-budgeted RP, and the decision to benchmark rather than replace.** It states the cost of the alternative (return estimation error, turnover, loss of the diversification guarantee) against the cost of the status quo (risk premium sacrificed, calm factors treated as real). This is the epistemic standard the rest of the audit should hold.
6. **The variance-entropy frontier as a concept** — `α=0` min-variance and `λ=0` max-entropy as the two endpoints — is the correct mental model even though its current implementation and selection rule are not. Keep it as a diagnostic; do not keep it as the α-calibration engine.

## Verdict

The mathematics of U10 is the strongest part of this repository: the principal-basis transformation is necessary and exact, the contribution decomposition is additive and exact, the gradient is correct and tested against finite differences, the tilted-entropy extension is a true `−KL` and reduces correctly, and the code explicitly refuses to let the idiosyncratic block inflate ENB. Where the unit fails is not in the formulas but in the passage from formula to operating point: the α price is overwritten by a data-dependent gradient-norm rescaling, the selection rule prefers minimum variance among α that clear a target floored at 2 effective bets, the frontier evaluates an entropy different from the one it maximises, and the OOS path rebalances with a different objective than the one used to seed the portfolio. The single real-data run is the proof: ENB 2.70, 81% of systematic risk in the market factor, α at the smallest positive grid point. The two-layer idiosyncratic entropy is a well-implemented but mis-justified addition — it equalises residual risk across stocks, not within-factor exposure, and its weight is calibrated against a quantity it does not measure. Structurally, most of the surrounding machinery (gradient normalisation, ENB elbow, α grid, multi-start SCA) exists to manage a scalarisation that a constrained or log-barrier risk-budgeting formulation would make unnecessary, and the repo already contains the convex two-stage alternative it should promote.

Score: 6/10
