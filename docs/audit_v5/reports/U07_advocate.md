## Five questions

1. **Purpose and original intent.** Training must turn overlapping 504-day windows into a generative encoder whose latent coordinates are stable enough to be used as risk exposures, and must do so without leaking the future: "The encoder learns a broad vocabulary of risk patterns across **all** historical regimes… Current relevance is handled at inference" (DVT:594-596). The curriculum is not cosmetic: synchronous+stratified batching exists because "the co-movement loss is not computable without temporal synchronization" (ISD:1573). Early stopping is the real convergence criterion, "Monitor the **validation ELBO** on the temporal validation set (last 2 years of training data, with embargo)" (DVT:616), and its argmin "is denoted $E^*$ and serves as the fixed stopping point for the Phase B deployment run" (DVT:625). Retraining is a full refit on an expanding window, quarterly, with daily fine-tuning explicitly rejected (DVT:630-640).

2. **State of the art for this objective.** Adam/AdamW with β=(0.9,0.999), ε=1e-8 is the standard for VAEs (DVT:602 cites Kingma & Ba 2015, FactorVAE, RVRAE, Diffusion-VAE). Beyond the spec, SOTA would add: a short LR warmup before plateau decay (Adam's bias-corrected first steps are the least stable at η₀=5e-3); KL annealing done properly (Bowman et al. 2016; β-VAE, Higgins et al. 2017) — here only Mode F, not Mode P; **purged/embargoed** validation for overlapping windows (López de Prado 2018), not just a date split; checkpoint selection on a downstream criterion rather than pure reconstruction ELBO; multiple seeds per fold with dispersion reported. Synchronized cross-sectional batch sampling is not standard VAE practice but is a defensible domain adaptation; nothing in the code claims otherwise.

3. **What prevents it.** Not compute: one real fold trained 164 epochs on ~739 stocks × 24y (checkpoint filename, `results/diagnostic/checkpoints/`). The binding obstacles are architectural: (a) the curriculum is denominated in `max_epochs` (loss.py:633-642) so early stopping truncates Phases 2-3 — in the only real run, $E^*=164$ of 500 means Phase 3 never runs and λ_co≈0.45 at deployment; (b) Phase B does not do what the spec says (train on all data for $E^*$ with no validation) — it holds out the last 10% of *array positions* (pipeline.py:1888-1890), which in a stock-major window array is a stock holdout, and then `restore_best` re-selects an epoch (trainer.py:841); (c) $E^*$ itself is replaced by the median of prior folds (phase_b.py:43-48) and the recorded `e_star` is not updated in that path (pipeline.py:1923-1924). Apparent-only obstacles: the 100/250/500 epoch ceilings are not convergence targets, so raising `MAX_EPOCHS` from 100 to 500 bought nothing except a longer curriculum denominator; and the "2-year validation set" is not an out-of-sample guard while it overlaps training windows by ~503 of 504 days.

4. **Real vs self-imposed constraints.** Real: 504-day windows at stride 21 leave ~15 effectively independent windows per stock (`AGGREGATION_HALF_LIFE=60` rationale, dashboard.ipynb:268 implies the same overlap logic); finite GPU/Colab memory; non-stationarity; delisting; point-in-time universe. Self-imposed: Phase B's 90/10 split; median-of-prior-folds $E^*$; curriculum fractions multiplied by the epoch ceiling; η₀=5e-3 (50× the spec's 1e-4, DVT:606); patience 20/50 and factor 0.75 vs 10/5/0.5 (DVT:1029-1030); batch 512 vs 256; the hardcoded sampler seed 42 (batching.py:50); no `torch.manual_seed` anywhere in `src/` or `scripts/`.

5. **Remove, merge, move.** Remove: Phase B's internal validation split *and* the `restore_best()` call in that path (train exactly $E^*$ epochs on 100% of windows — this loses no strength, because the deployment run is supposed to be validation-free, phase_b.py:4-5). Remove the median-$E^*$ substitution as a silent default, or make it an explicit logged parameter and record the *deployed* epoch. Remove the dead checks (`warn_if_loss_component_imbalance` reads keys that do not exist, trainer.py:788-789; `check_training_sanity` is imported but never called, pipeline.py:95). Merge: the λ_co schedule, sampler mode switch and epoch budget into one schedule object keyed to an *absolute* epoch budget (or to an expected $E^*$), so Phase 3 no longer depends on nobody pressing the early-stopping brake. Move: checkpoint selection from per-epoch val ELBO to a cheap downstream criterion (conditioning/variance-ratio on a purged slice), keeping ELBO as a baseline — this is a move, not an addition.

## Core strengths to preserve
1. **One source of truth for the curriculum.** `get_lambda_co` drives both the loss weight and the sampler mode; any redesign must keep that coupling, or the co-movement objective and the batch geometry will silently desynchronize (loss.py:633-642; trainer.py:757-762).
2. **A γ-free, λ-free validation ELBO (INV-011).** This is the only metric that is comparable across the three curriculum phases and across loss modes; it must survive any redesign, even if it stops being the *selection* criterion (trainer.py:586-636; ISD:1565).
3. **Warmup protection that understands why it exists.** The Mode-F path refuses to compare ELBOs computed under different β, and re-seeds early stopping with a valid checkpoint at β=1. This is a subtle, correctly solved problem and should be generalized to any non-stationary loss schedule (trainer.py:736,810-825).
4. **Numerical survivability as a first-class feature.** Post-step σ² clamp, gradient clipping, NaN-loss skip, NaN-batch exclusion in validation, and restore-best together mean one bad batch cannot destroy a fold; for daily equity data at η₀=5e-3 this is not optional (trainer.py:399-445, 492-503, 605-635).
5. **Leak-free, per-fold stratification.** Strata are rebuilt from returns ending at `train_end`, so batching diversity does not import future information; the sampler asserts index bounds and never samples across blocks (pipeline.py:1269,1296-1300,1883-1885; batching.py:150-151).
6. **Full refit, no warm start.** Retraining from a fresh initialization each fold protects the interpretability of the latent basis; a redesign that "saves time" by warm-starting across folds would trade away the fixed-meaning guarantee of B (DVT:676).

## Verdict
The training unit is the strongest part of the implementation I have seen in this repository: the loop is faithful to a coherent spec, the curriculum has a single source of truth, INV-011 is implemented exactly, early stopping is NaN-safe and restores the right checkpoint, Mode-F warmup is handled with more care than the spec requires, and the robustness envelope (clipping, σ² clamp, skip-NaN, restore-best) is appropriate for financial data at a high learning rate. The weaknesses are not in the mathematics but in the *contract between training and deployment*: the curriculum is denominated in the epoch ceiling rather than in the training actually performed, so the one real run stopped mid-Phase 2 with the co-movement scaffold still active; Phase B holds out the last 10% of *stocks* and then re-selects an epoch, contradicting its own "no validation set" contract; $E^*$ is silently replaced by a median of prior folds and the reported `e_star` is not the deployed epoch; and the validation split shares ~483 of 504 days with training, so early stopping is not measuring temporal generalization. Add the absence of any `torch.manual_seed` and five training knobs that differ between the notebook and the run artifact, and the unit's guarantees are weaker in practice than its design suggests. The design is sound; the wiring around it is what needs redesign.

Score: 6/10
