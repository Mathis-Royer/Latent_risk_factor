## Five questions

**1. Purpose and original intent.** The unit is supposed to answer "whether the strategy *actually* works without hindsight" (DVT:862) by a three-segment expanding walk-forward: training `[start, t_k−2yr]`, temporal validation `[t_k−2yr, t_k]` used only for early stopping/LR, a 21-day embargo, and ~6-month OOS blocks (DVT:870–878), ~34 folds (DVT:866), a Phase A HP selection and a Phase B deployment retrain on all data for `E*` epochs (DVT:894–900), a 3-year holdout as "the only uncontaminated evaluation" (DVT:880), and a benchmark-relative decision with paired Wilcoxon + Holm (ISD:2394–2405). The stated intent is sound and unusually explicit. The implemented artefact is a different protocol; see CRI-01/02/03.

**2. State of the art for this objective.** Purged/embargoed walk-forward with an *inner* validation loop that never touches the outer test block (López de Prado 2018, ch. 7 and 12); rolling-origin evaluation for time series (Bergmeir & Benítez 2012); combinatorial purged CV to obtain a *distribution of backtest paths* rather than one path (López de Prado 2018, ch. 12); block/stationary bootstrap or White (2000) Reality Check / Hansen (2005) SPA for dependent, overlapping strategy returns; deflated Sharpe / minimum-backtest-length corrections for selection among many configurations (Bailey & López de Prado 2014; Arnott, Harvey & Markowitz 2019); a pre-registered, once-only holdout with a stated minimum detectable effect. The current unit cites none of these and implements none of the dependence corrections.

**3. What prevents it from reaching that.** Not data, not math: the architecture has **no validation-time quantity that predicts portfolio performance**. The only inner-loop signals are validation ELBO and AU (pipeline:1457–1482), which are not the objective; consequently the designers pushed HP comparison onto the outer OOS (DVT:929), which destroys the outer set. Everything else follows: the Phase A downstream pipeline is not built at all and its output fields are placeholders (pipeline:1487–1497); the EP-vs-PCA gate is a constant 0.5 (pipeline:1496) while the real EP is 0.219/0.356 (`vae_fold_metrics.csv:1-2`; `diagnostic_report.md:56-61`); the report/decision layer never gets more than one fold and therefore has no test to run (`report.txt:10-16`).

*Steelman.* (i) The design is honest about the holdout being "the only uncontaminated evaluation" — it knows the WF is selection-contaminated. (ii) Phase A is cheap (+25%) and per-fold adaptation is more realistic than one global config. (iii) `E*` transfer avoids a validation set in deployment, matching production. (iv) 34 folds do cover multiple regimes, which a single split cannot.

*Rebuttal.* (i) If the WF is contaminated, it cannot be the evidence base, yet it is the only evidence base that exists; the holdout has never been run on Tiingo (the only real-data run is `run_direct`, CRI-03). (ii) Phase A is not run at all in the executable path (`notebooks/dashboard.ipynb:1144,1150`) and, when enabled, does not build the pipeline the doc requires (ISD:2257–2258), so its "adaptation" is a comparison of ELBO/AU, not of strategy quality. (iii) The E* transfer is not implemented correctly (MAJ-21) and its justification is a non sequitur (MAJ-15). (iv) The regimes are *adjacent* folds of one history; 2008 occupies a handful of consecutive folds, so "crisis vs calm" is one or two episodes, not a sample (MAJ-14).

**4. Real vs self-imposed constraints.** *Real:* one realised price history; non-stationarity; 31.2 years of data ⇒ at most ~34 non-overlapping 6-month blocks and far fewer effective independent ones; `log N` folds cannot be increased by design; CPU/GPU budget for retraining. *Self-imposed:* the 6-month block length, 21-day embargo, 3-year holdout, 2-year validation, expanding window, per-fold Phase A selection, `E*` median transfer, the exact composite score and its λs, the choice to compute H from the model rather than from realised contributions, a single seed, the nominal `total_years` (MAJ-11), 63-day OOS rebalancing while the doc says "held fixed" (MAJ-12), and the median/win-count decision rule (CRI-04). Only the first four constraints are binding; the rest are conventions that could be otherwise and are not defended.

**5. What should be removed, merged or moved.** **Remove:** the Phase A-per-fold selection on OOS (CRI-01); the Phase B "deployment" step in its current form (MAJ-05/07); the `skip_phase_a` dual mode that makes "Phase B" mean two incompatible things; the A/B/C/D/E scenario layer (CRI-04); the nominal-horizon holdout (MAJ-11); the 63-day OOS rebalancing *or* the "portfolio held fixed" claim — one must go. **Merge:** the two entropy normalizations (ln AU in `phase_a.py:45`, ln K in `metrics.py:684`); the two meanings of "validation" (early stopping vs HP comparison) into one explicitly-defined inner window; the statistical test and the decision into one path instead of test-in-reporting / decision-in-`selection.py`. **Move:** HP selection into an inner loop over each fold's training data; the VAE-vs-PCA gate from post-hoc reporting into selection, with `EP_PCA` computed point-in-time; the embargo invariant from documentation into the assertion (`folds.py:91` currently passes `min_embargo_days=0`). **Delete:** the unused Bonferroni helpers or wire them into selection (MIN-24).

## Root-cause hypotheses

**R1 — No inner objective aligned with the product.** The only validation-time signals are ELBO and AU; neither predicts portfolio quality. So selection was pushed onto the outer OOS (DVT:929), which is why the "nested" label is false (CRI-01), why Phase A fakes its outputs (CRI-02), why the EP gate is a constant (MAJ-20), and why the primary metric is never comparable across models (MAJ-22). *Alternatives:* (a) define an explicit validation-window surrogate — realised/predicted variance ratio, EP vs a point-in-time PCA, latent stability — and select HP on it, reserving OOS for evaluation; trade-off: surrogate may not rank configs the same way as portfolio performance, but it is falsifiable and cheap. (b) Full nested walk-forward: an inner expanding loop inside each outer fold's training data; trade-off: ~4–10× compute, and inner OOS blocks are short in early folds. (c) Pre-register one configuration from theory and use OOS only once; trade-off: no adaptivity, but valid inference and a real holdout.

**R2 — One backtest path treated as a sample.** Expanding-window WF produces a single path; 34 fold scores are pseudo-replicates. This generates MAJ-14 (invalid paired inference), the "distribution of OOS results" rhetoric (DVT:866), the crisis/calm decomposition with 1–2 episodes (DVT:961), and the willingness to draw a scenario conclusion from n=1 (CRI-04). *Alternatives:* (a) combinatorial purged CV (CPCV) over the same 34 cut dates to produce many paths and a genuine distribution; trade-off: compute and a more complex reporting unit. (b) Block/stationary bootstrap over calendar time with an explicit effective-sample-size statement; trade-off: wider intervals, possibly no significant result — which is the honest outcome. (c) Keep one path and replace significance claims with a minimum detectable effect/precision statement; trade-off: no ranking of close alternatives.

**R3 — The harness is aspirational while the runnable path bypasses it.** Phase A/B, E*, holdout and the required tests exist as code-shaped interfaces, but the executable path sets `skip_phase_a=True` and calls `run_direct`, while placeholders and dead branches fill the gaps (CRI-02/03, MAJ-05/06/07, MAJ-19, MIN-18/24). This is the direct cause of the doc-vs-code drift the whole audit keeps finding. *Alternatives:* (a) delete the unexecuted Phase A/B and state one honest protocol, so the report cannot claim it; trade-off: loses the (currently fictional) adaptivity. (b) Implement and make CI fail if the executed protocol differs from the manifest; trade-off: engineering cost, but the artifacts become auditable. (c) Make every report print a signed protocol manifest (schedule, checkpoint cutoff, code hash) and refuse to emit a scenario label without it; trade-off: none beyond discipline.

**R4 — Calendar/horizon parameters are conventions, not derivations.** `total_years`, 21 days, 3 years, 6 months, 2 years and 63 days are round constants set independently in config, notebook and docs (MAJ-11/12, MIN-16). The data span is 31.2 years, so the nominal 30/35/40-year horizons each imply a different, partly non-existent holdout. *Alternatives:* (a) derive the schedule from the actual panel (`data_start`, `data_end`) and freeze it in the run manifest; trade-off: schedules differ across data refreshes, so results are not comparable unless the manifest is reported. (b) Tie the embargo/purge to the window length T (purge overlapping windows) instead of a round month; trade-off: much larger gaps, fewer folds. (c) Make the holdout a fixed calendar block chosen ex ante (e.g. 2022-01-01→data end) and never regenerate it; trade-off: the block is no longer exactly 3 years as new data arrives.

**R5 — The metric stack measures the model against itself.** `H_norm_oos`, EP and var-ratio are all computed from the same B/Σ_z estimated in training; benchmarks have no H (MAJ-09/10/22). The observed H_norm 0.5337 sits far below the doc's own "typical 0.80–0.95" band (DVT:937) yet is reported "OK" (`diagnostic_report.md:107`), and the var-ratio is 2.742 vs a [0.5, 2.0] health band and [0.8, 1.2] target (`diagnostic_report.md:57`; DVT:910). *Alternatives:* (a) define the primary OOS metric as realised risk contributions under an external covariance (sample or Ledoit-Wolf), not the model's; trade-off: noisier, but not self-fulfilling. (b) Report predicted-vs-realised entropy as a pair (a calibration gap), not a single "entropy" number; trade-off: two numbers, harder to rank. (c) Make the decision metric benchmark-relative by construction (ΔH against PCA under the same external covariance), so it exists for all models; trade-off: needs PCA factor decompositions on the same dates.

## Verdict

The specification of U12 is the strongest part of the project: expanding walk-forward, a temporal validation block, an explicit embargo, a deployment phase, a holdout and a benchmark-relative decision are the right *ingredients*. The unit as implemented is not a validation protocol. It selects hyperparameters on the same OOS window it reports (and the code's Phase A cannot even compute the selection score, so it would select on grid order if enabled); the only real-data artefact comes from a different, single-split code path using a pretrained checkpoint, with one fold and every significance test skipped; and the decision layer converts that absence of evidence into "Scenario C — Adopt PCA" through a fallback branch. The fold schedule is decoupled from the data span, the OOS is rebalanced although the protocol says held fixed, the entropy normalisation differs between selection and reporting, the "primary" metric is unavailable for benchmarks and is computed from the model's own covariance, and the required Phase B invariant is both violated in the pipeline and mis-tested in CI. These are not local bugs: they follow from the absence of an inner objective, from treating one path as a sample, and from a harness that exists in prose but is bypassed at runtime. With the current artefacts, the strategy has **no valid out-of-sample evidence at all**; the V1/V2 Sharpe numbers are documents, and the one real-data run is n=1 under a different protocol. A rebuild should delete Phase A/B and the scenario layer, adopt nested selection on a risk-model surrogate or pre-registration, produce multiple backtest paths (CPCV or block bootstrap), and make the primary metric realised and benchmark-relative.

Score: 2/10
