## Five questions

**1. Purpose and original intent.** The spec states the intent without ambiguity: *"The loss function determines what the latent space captures"* (`docs/DVT_strategie_facteurs_risque_latents_v4_1.md:440`). Four distinct jobs are bundled into one scalar: (i) a Gaussian **reconstruction** objective on the window, deliberately a per-element *mean* so that a learned scalar σ² is a per-element residual variance *"directly interpretable on normalized data"* (`:448`); (ii) a **KL** term whose per-dimension value doubles as the factor-counting device — *"Dimensions with marginal KL > 0.01 nats are active units"* (`:404`); (iii) a **co-movement** term that supplies the cross-sectional structure a per-sample autoencoder has no incentive to learn (`:496`); (iv) a **crisis reweighting** that counteracts the ~2–5% frequency of stress windows (`:546`). MOD-004 calls this *"the mathematical core of the VAE — any error here silently invalidates training"* (`docs/ISD_vae_latent_risk_factors.md:1293`). The validation ELBO is a fifth, deliberately separate object: γ and λ_co are excluded because they are training tricks, σ² is included because it is a model parameter (`:622`).

**2. What a state-of-the-art version would look like.** For the *decoder* half, the unit is already at SOTA: a learned scalar observation variance is exactly the Dai & Wipf / Lucas et al. construction the doc cites (`:464`, refs [12][13] at `:1600–1602`), and the profile identity σ²\* = MSE is the correct parameterisation of the recon/KL trade-off. The two things SOTA would add are (a) **per-feature σ²** (returns and log-vol have very different predictability; a single scalar forces a mis-specified likelihood — the spec forbids this by fiat, `ISD:1096`), and (b) a **direct cross-sectional objective** instead of a pairwise-distance proxy. The canonical reference for the latter is Gu, Kelly & Xiu (2021), *Autoencoder Asset Pricing Models* — already cited as [11] (`DVT:1598`) but not used to shape the objective. The doc itself names the right fix and gets the diagnosis right (`docs/strategic_paths_post_v2.md:168–173`): compute μ for the whole universe and regress the cross-section. Its co-movement term is, in effect, a 2-year-averaged, rank-correlation version of that — a proxy for the thing you actually want.

**3. What prevents reaching it — and which obstacles are only apparent.** The blocking obstacle is *not* the loss arithmetic (that is correct, tested, and faithfully assembled). It is that the co-movement target is `g(ρ)=1−ρ` on Spearman correlation computed over overlapping 2-year windows (`DVT:500–504`), whereas the downstream object is the *date-by-date* cross-sectional covariance used to build B (`DVT:714`). Average pairwise time-series correlation does not imply good per-date exposure vectors, so even a perfectly minimised L_co does not guarantee the property the risk model needs. **Apparent** obstacles: (i) "σ² is one more fragile hyperparameter" — it is a profiled parameter with no schedule and no tuning knob, and both clamps are inert at these data scales (1e-4/10 vs MSE≈0.5); (ii) "the D/(2σ²)≈1000 coefficient makes the loss numerically unstable" — it is a constant scale and Adam is scale-invariant, and the code asserts finiteness and KL≥0 every batch (`loss.py:364–368`); (iii) "the model cannot see the cross-section at all" — it can, through the shared encoder plus the co-movement term; the problem is the *shape* of the signal, not its absence.

**4. Real vs self-imposed constraints.** Real: each stock must be encoded in an independent forward pass to keep universe flexibility (`DVT:542`); the reconstruction target has exactly D = T·F elements; pairwise correlation estimates from finite histories are rank-limited and noisy. Self-imposed: scalar (not per-feature) σ², `INV-002` (`ISD:1096`); cosine distance as the metric, with a linear `1−ρ` calibration that is stated but never justified (`DVT:504`); λ_co expressed as an absolute coefficient while the code silently multiplies it by D/2 (`loss.py:386–389`); the three mutually-exclusive modes as a *hard* partition (`loss.py:346–348`); the cross-sectional loss restricted to the batch, which the doc itself identifies as the flawed V2 design (`strategic_paths:171`); and the three-phase curriculum, which exists only because the co-movement term needs synchronous batches (`DVT:518`).

**5. What should be removed, merged or moved.** Remove the **batch-level cross-sectional R² loss** outright: it is documented as *"Confirmed harmful — corrupted encoder"* (`strategic_paths:291`), it is off by default (`config.py:242`), and it is structurally incapable of measuring what it claims (below, U06-ADV-18). Remove the **three-phase co-movement scaffolding and its batching subsystem** (`batching.py` synchronous/stratified sampler + `compute_strata` k-means) *if and only if* the cross-sectional constraint is moved into the architecture/decoder — the scaffolding is the only reason synchronous batching, strata, `delta_sync`, `max_pairs` and the curriculum fractions exist. Remove **`feature_weights`** (undocumented, empirically neutral, `strategic_paths:306`, and it breaks INV-001's σ² interpretation). Merge **γ into the same conversation as σ²**: they are not orthogonal knobs; σ²\* is the γ-weighted MSE, so a single documented identity covers both. Move the **active-unit decision** out of the loss's KL scale into inference (cross-unit U08); the 0.01-nat cut is an artefact of the D-scaling, not a statistical criterion. What must *not* be removed: the profiled-σ² Mode P, the per-element-mean/D-scaling convention, and the γ/λ-co-free validation ELBO.

## Core strengths to preserve
1. **The D-scaling convention and per-element-mean MSE** (`loss.py:51–52,66,352`). It is the difference between a working VAE and instant posterior collapse, it makes σ² a data-free hyperparameter, and it is guarded three ways. Any redesign that touches the decoder must keep this bookkeeping exactly.
2. **Profiled scalar σ² as Mode P** (`DVT:484`, `loss.py:397–401`). This is the statistically correct recon/KL balancer, it removes an entire hyperparameter dimension, and its clamps are inert — the strongest single design decision in the unit.
3. **The γ/λ-co-free validation ELBO** (`loss.py:649–701`). Keeping training tricks out of the model-selection criterion is what makes early stopping defensible; a redesign that "cleans up" by putting γ into the val loss would destroy it.
4. **Point-in-time crisis labelling** (`crisis.py:133–194`). Expanding-window threshold, dates-only filtering, per-window fractions — no leakage path.
5. **The co-movement *machinery*, if the objective survives**: synchronous blocks, stock→window stratum mapping, ranks computed once per batch, symmetric pair enumeration, target under `no_grad`. If the term is replaced by a universe-wide cross-sectional objective, this is the plumbing that gets reused.
6. **The failure record** (`strategic_paths:287–306`) and the **monitoring design** (`DVT:470–476`: linear-VAE σ² reference, AU vs Bai–Ng, EP vs PCA). The unit's own falsification criteria are better than its results.
7. **Mode exclusivity enforced in code** (`loss.py:346–348`). The reasoning at `DVT:480` (learned σ² and β address the same degree of freedom; combining them gives unpredictable compensation) is correct and is the right kind of guardrail.

## Verdict
This is a genuinely well-built unit, and its weaknesses are almost entirely in *what it optimises*, not in *how*. The Gaussian assembly, the D bookkeeping, the profiled σ², the KL, the curriculum, the point-in-time crisis labelling and the γ/λ-co-free validation ELBO are mathematically correct, faithfully implemented, guarded by asserts and covered by the required tests plus extras; the literature is accurately cited and the failed experiments are honestly recorded. The correct redesign is not to repair any of this but to *delete* the proxy objective together with its batching/curriculum/strata apparatus, delete the cross-sectional R² branch and `feature_weights`, keep Mode P and the ELBO discipline untouched, and move the cross-sectional constraint where it belongs — into an objective or decoder that sees the whole cross-section at each date. From the advocate's seat, this unit is sound in its mathematics and mis-aimed in its cross-sectional ambition.
