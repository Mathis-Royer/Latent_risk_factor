## Five questions

**1. Purpose and original intent (cite the spec).**
Spec §4.7 defines one diversification metric: "Entropy of principal factor risk contributions" (DVT:682). Raw contributions can be negative when factors are correlated, so the spec rotates to the principal basis where `c'_k = (β'_{p,k})² · λ_k ≥ 0` (DVT:686) and maximises Shannon entropy (DVT:690). Stated purpose: "Maximum entropy = equal risk from each principal factor = no dominant risk concentration" (DVT:706). Philosophy §7 sharpens the claim: exposure is "inversely proportional to the square root of the factor's variance" (philosophy:116) and the portfolio is "exposed to each risk in the exact proportion that makes its contribution to total portfolio risk identical to all others" (philosophy:124). The declared hierarchy is "risk minimization is the primary goal, factorial diversification is the means" (DVT:765), with `max -λ w'Σw + αH` (DVT:769) and α set at "the elbow" of the variance–entropy frontier (DVT:814). §8 adds a *second* diversification level implemented by constraints ("dual diversification", philosophy:136–150). Nothing in the spec mentions an idiosyncratic entropy layer, an eigenvalue split, PC1 exclusion, `n_signal`, or an ENB target. Those are code-era constructs, retrospectively documented in the lit review.

**2. State of the art for this objective.**
The closest correct formulations are: (i) Meucci's diversification distribution / ENB over *all* principal portfolios, with the market component either explicitly constrained or neutralised rather than deleted (Meucci 2009, the spec's own ref. [39]); (ii) Roncalli–Weisang factor risk budgeting, where budgets are imposed as *constraints* in risk-contribution space and the solution is characterised by RC_k ∝ b_k — not by adding a tilted entropy to a variance penalty; (iii) constrained min-variance with an explicit ENB or maximum-risk-share constraint (Maillard–Roncalli–Teïletche 2010; Clarke–de Silva–Thorley 2011/2013); (iv) a benchmark-relative/tracking-error formulation if the intent is to diversify *non-market* risk while holding beta (the correct treatment once one accepts "the market is not a diversifiable bet"); (v) for the idiosyncratic layer, an explicit constraint on idiosyncratic variance share or on holdings per factor, not an entropy over stock-level contributions. None of these mixes entropies with different supports and units using a free weight.

**3. What prevents the current unit reaching it (evidenced).**
Four things, all evidenced: (a) the market factor is deleted from the entropy objective (pipeline.py:2294), so the dominant risk is neither diversified nor controlled — it is simply absent from the objective; (b) each entropy layer is normalised within itself (entropy.py:65–79, 173–186), which makes the combined objective invariant to the systematic/idiosyncratic split; (c) the "frontier" no longer exists as a frontier — α is selected to hit a data-derived ENB target with arbitrary constants and then min-variance is preferred among qualifying points (frontier.py:503–537, 403–407; pipeline.py:2612–2621); (d) the reported diversification metric is computed with mutually inconsistent denominators (diagnostics.py:891–907; metrics.py:675–688), so the evidence used to validate the unit does not measure what the unit optimises.

**4. Real vs self-imposed constraints.**
Real: long-only, fully invested, per-name caps, ~788 names / 30y daily data, estimation error in a 27×27 Σ_z, and the empirical fact that a long-only equity portfolio cannot reduce market risk contribution to 1/AU without leverage or beta hedging. Self-imposed: excluding PC1 from H instead of constraining beta; per-block normalisation; the idio entropy layer; the free `entropy_idio_weight`; the adaptive ENB target (0.5 / 0.7 / floor 2.0); the α>0 floor; the uniform-vs-proportional mode ambiguity; using the construction objective as the primary evaluation metric; reporting H only for the VAE; the "variance-entropy frontier" apparatus.

**5. What should be removed, merged or moved.**
Remove: the idiosyncratic entropy layer (it duplicates asset-level ERC — see CRI-11) and replace it with an explicit idio-variance-share or holdings-per-factor constraint; the adaptive ENB target, the α-floor and the two-phase frontier machinery (it is a target search, not a frontier); gradient clipping at 10 as a *global* device (keep it, if at all, inside the solver, never inside α normalisation); the three competing `H_norm` definitions.
Merge: factor and idiosyncratic entropies into one objective on *shares of total variance*, with the market as one bin and idiosyncratic risk as one aggregate bin; VAE and PCA benchmarks onto one identical objective and budget mode.
Move: the market factor out of the "deleted from H" bucket into an explicit benchmark-relative/beta constraint at the optimizer level; α out of the in-sample optimisation loop into a pre-registered constant (or delete α and impose ENB as a constraint); diversification evaluation to an asset-level ENB/DR reported for *all* benchmarks; performance evaluation to drawdown/crisis/beta-relative metrics rather than the objective itself.

## Findings

*Steelman (strongest pro-design case):* the principal-basis rotation is mathematically sound (it guarantees non-negative contributions and a well-defined entropy, DVT:684–692); the gradient is correct and verifiably vanishes at equal contributions (DVT:700–704); the entropy–variance objective is a defensible way to trade robustness against risk; the two-layer split is a genuine attempt to prevent dimensionality from swamping the factor signal; and the benchmark suite is unusually honest for a research repo. *Rebuttal:* sound local mathematics do not rescue a mis-specified global objective. The realised portfolio puts 81% of its systematic risk in one factor and achieves 2.7 effective bets; the unit's own reported "primary" numbers come from three different denominators; and the layer-normalisation choice makes the objective blind to the split it claims to control. The elegance of the gradient is not evidence for the objective.

## Root-cause hypotheses

**R1 — Component-normalised objective applied to total-risk claims.** The objective is defined on a *normalised sub-block* (12 non-market factors, then a separate normalised idio block) but the narrative and the metric are about *total* portfolio risk. Once each block is normalised, its share of total risk drops out, and deleting PC1 removes the dominant term entirely. This single cause explains CRI-01, 02, 09, 11, 18, 21, 22 — i.e. most of the unit's symptoms. Structural alternatives: (a) **benchmark-relative formulation** — hold beta at 1 (or at benchmark) by construction and diversify tracking error; trade-off: requires a benchmark and explicit beta handling, and changes the product from "absolute risk" to "relative risk"; (b) **single variance-share objective** — one entropy over all AU factors plus one aggregate idio bin, using absolute contributions to total variance; trade-off: reintroduces a dominant market bin, so the optimum will be far from equal and the metric becomes less flattering (which is the honest outcome); (c) **constraint instead of objective** — minimise variance subject to an explicit maximum single-factor risk share and a maximum idio share; trade-off: needs feasibility analysis (long-only caps may make a low market share infeasible) but is falsifiable and directly interpretable.

**R2 — Metric circularity and denominator drift.** The objective, the α-selection target and the "primary" evaluation metric are the same quantity with three incompatible normalisations (ln AU, ln K, ln n_signal) and a mismatched numerator. This explains CRI-06, 07, 08, 24 and the "OK" health check. Structural alternatives: (a) **separate construction from evaluation** — construction uses ENB only as a constraint; evaluation uses drawdown, crisis-period return, beta-relative return and asset-level ENB/DR reported for every benchmark; trade-off: the strategy may look worse, but the evidence becomes admissible; (b) **one metric, one basis, one denominator** — pre-register ln(AU) over all AU factors and report it everywhere, including benchmarks; trade-off: requires recomputing all history and accepting that the current 0.30 is poor; (c) **drop the scalar** — report the full contribution vector / top-1/top-3 shares; trade-off: less quotable, more honest.

**R3 — Diversification across weakly identified directions.** Equalising risk across eigen-directions of a shrunk, truncated covariance treats estimation noise as a risk source: low eigenvalues receive the largest exposures under uniform budgeting (philosophy:116 explicitly celebrates this), and directions within near-degenerate clusters are unidentified. This explains CRI-05, 12, 16, 17. Structural alternatives: (a) **budget only well-identified directions** — require λ_k above a statistical threshold (or a signal-to-noise ratio) before including a factor in the entropy, and handle the rest as an aggregate "residual systematic" bin; trade-off: an extra threshold to defend, but it is testable; (b) **shrink the objective, not just the covariance** — optimise a robust/penalised version of H so that small eigenvalue errors cannot dominate; trade-off: more computation and a robustness parameter; (c) **abandon the eigenbasis** — use economically identified or hierarchical (HRP-style) risk sources where "equal risk" has a stable meaning; trade-off: gives up the "no a priori bias" premise (U01).

**R4 — Parameter and document drift substituting for theory.** Magic constants (0.5, 0.7, floor 2.0, clip 10, idio 0.05/0.2/0.0), two budget modes, an α floor, and stale documents that describe a different strategy from the one that runs. This explains CRI-03, 04, 13, 14, 15, 19, 23. Structural alternatives: (a) **one mode, pre-registered constants with provenance** — delete the adaptive target and the proportional mode, record every constant and its justification in one config; trade-off: loses the ability to tune per fold, which is the point; (b) **treat documents as executable specs** — generate the config summary and the claimed objective from the code at run time, and fail the run if they disagree; trade-off: engineering effort, but it makes audit findings like CRI-04/14 impossible; (c) **delete the Sortino-budget proposal from the roadmap** until the basis/market problem is fixed; adding a second estimator (trailing factor Sortino) to an unidentifiable objective multiplies estimation error, as the doc's own B7–B9 acknowledge (strategy_comparison.md:78–80).

## Verdict

The mathematics *inside* `entropy.py` is largely correct: the rotation to the principal basis is the right fix for negative contributions, the gradient derivation is consistent with the code, and the tilted-entropy gradient is (contrary to first appearances) algebraically right. But the unit's theory is not the theory that runs, and the theory that runs does not deliver the claim. The market factor — 81% of systematic risk — is deleted from the objective with no beta constraint replacing it; the two entropy layers are separately normalised, making the objective blind to the very systematic/idiosyncratic split it purports to control; the "idiosyncratic layer" is asset-level ERC in disguise; the "variance–entropy frontier" has been replaced by a search for an arbitrary ENB floor followed by min-variance; and the reported "primary" diversification number is computed with a numerator over 27 bins and a denominator of ln 12. The empirical record is consistent with this diagnosis: an ENB of 2.70, 81% of systematic risk in one factor, a Sharpe below four of six benchmarks, and a drawdown worse than min-variance. The unit's strongest defence — that it equalises the factors it chooses to equalise — is true and irrelevant, because the choice of factors is what removes the risk it claims to diversify. This unit requires architectural re-specification (market in the constraint layer, one variance-share objective, metric separated from objective), not parameter tuning; the proposed Sortino extension should be dropped until then.

Score: 3/10
