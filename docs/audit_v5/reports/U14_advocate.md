## Five questions

**1. Purpose and original intent.** The unit is a falsification apparatus, not a scoring hobby. `docs/Benchmark_latent_risk_factor.md:5` states the governing principle: "la complexité du VAE n'est justifiée que si elle produit un bénéfice OOS mesurable et statistiquement significatif par rapport à des alternatives plus simples, sur les mêmes données, avec les mêmes contraintes, évaluées par les mêmes métriques." §3 defines three metric layers (primary/diagnostic/risk-model), §4 the paired per-fold Wilcoxon + effect size + Holm correction + regime split, §6 the A–E decision grid. The intended output is a *pre-specified, reversible* verdict: continue, switch to PCA, switch to 1/N, or build a two-regime system.

**2. State of the art for this objective.** Six upgrades over the current design, all standard in the forecast-evaluation literature: (i) compare the VAE against the *set* of benchmarks with a Model Confidence Set (Hansen, Lunde & Nason 2011) or the Reality Check / SPA test (White 2000; Hansen 2005), which exploits the correlation among the 24 tests and answers "is VAE in the MCS?" instead of 18 independent nulls; (ii) Romano–Wolf stepdown (2005) if FWER with dependent tests is wanted, or BH if FDR is acceptable — Holm is valid but dominates nothing here because the tests are strongly correlated; (iii) stationary/block bootstrap (Politis & Romano 1994) rather than an i.i.d. percentile bootstrap of 8–34 fold medians; (iv) deflated Sharpe / PBO / Minimum Backtest Length (Bailey & López de Prado 2014; Bailey, Borwein, López de Prado & Zhu 2017) driven by the *true* number of trials, not the number of benchmarks; (v) covariance-forecast evaluation as a test, not a band: VaR-exception POF/conditional-coverage tests (Kupiec 1995; Christoffersen 1998) or Diebold–Mariano (1995) on the variance forecast; (vi) a pre-registered single primary endpoint with an explicit "inconclusive" state and a specification-curve appendix (Simonsohn, Simmons & Nelson 2020), because a 4-metric × 6-benchmark × 2-regime grid with post-hoc scenario labels is itself a multiple-comparison machine.

**3. What prevents reaching it — and which obstacles are only apparent.** Real, evidenced blockers: the decisions never consult a p-value (selection.py:104–124, `p_threshold` at selection.py:79 is unused); the only objective-aligned metric is absent from every benchmark table, so at most 3 of 4 "primary" metrics can be tested (results/pca_factor_rp_fold_metrics.csv:1 vs results/vae_fold_metrics.csv:1); the holdout is inside the test family (pipeline.py:764–772, 782–788); the E\* selection criterion is degenerate by construction (pipeline.py:1494–1496); two decision surfaces disagree without arbitration (results/diagnostic/report.json:3–34 vs results/diagnostic/health_checks.csv:3); regime analysis is disabled at both call sites (pipeline.py:790, 1193); Scenario D and E cannot be emitted (selection.py:132–147). *Apparent* obstacles that are not the problem: Wilcoxon is the correct test here and is correctly implemented (statistical_tests.py:32–51) — its non-normality robustness is exactly what non-Gaussian fold deltas need; the percentile bootstrap is acceptable at K≈34 and the fragility at K=9 is a fold-count artifact, not an estimator defect; Holm is not "too conservative" in principle — with 34 folds its smallest threshold (0.05/24 = 0.0021) is attainable; zero power at 9 folds is a *sample-size* fact, not a correction flaw; and "the report shows the strategy fails" is not an obstacle at all, because report.json is a 20-stock, 1-epoch synthetic smoke test (results/config.json:3,9,31).

**4. Real vs self-imposed constraints.** Real: finite non-overlapping 6-month folds, non-Gaussian and serially dependent returns, correlation among metrics, non-stationary markets, and the fact that only one realisation of history exists. Self-imposed: the 4-metric primary set that includes an incomparable metric and excludes the crisis metric; the arbitrary 0–100 composite with ten hand-set weights (composite_scores.py:1548–1559; diagnostic.md:157–163); the fixed bands (0.8/1.2, 0.5/2.0, 60/75/90); the integer "wins" counter; the A–E alphabet with two unreachable states and a catch-all default; normalising entropy by model-specific capacity, which makes the primary metric non-comparable across models by design; and selecting E\* with a placeholder composite.

**5. What should be removed, merged or moved.** *Remove*: the 10-component 0–100 composite layer (composite_scores.py:1508–1647) — it is in-sample-only, never wired to the walk-forward report, renormalises its own denominator (1587–1600) and duplicates `run_health_checks`; the "wins" integer and the default-B branch; `score_hp_configs_with_correction`, which returns `significant = False` unconditionally (selection.py:222–243); `H_norm_oos` (ln K normalisation) as a primary metric until one entropy definition is shared by all models. *Merge*: the three decision surfaces (scenario matrix, diagnostic rules engine, health checks) into one decision object with documented precedence. *Move*: the holdout out of `vae_results` into a separate one-shot object; the scenario matrix from code into an evaluator that requires `n_tests > 0`; DSR/PBO into a trial-accounting layer that knows how many configurations were actually searched. Nothing above removes a strength: the paired design, Wilcoxon, Holm stepdown, effect sizes, per-fold median+IQR, the variance-ratio and EP metrics, the point-in-time VIX threshold and the log-space MDD all survive.

## Core strengths to preserve

1. **The paired per-fold design and the exact signed-rank test.** Non-overlapping OOS windows, one delta per fold, exact two-sided p-value, zeros dropped, and an explicit refusal below five non-zero pairs. Any redesign should keep this shape; the fix is more folds and a block bootstrap, not a different test.
2. **Holm stepdown, correctly implemented and unit-tested.** `alpha/(m−rank)` with the cascade stop is right. What must be replaced is the *caller* (reporting.py:107), not the procedure.
3. **Effect sizes with CIs reported alongside p-values**, and skipped tests that state their reason. This is the reporting contract a redesign should enforce everywhere.
4. **The benchmark ladder itself** (1/N → inv-vol → LW min-var → ERC → PCA-FRP → PCA+vol). It is a well-posed component-wise ablation with a documented 1/N null, and it is the only part of this unit that directly answers "does the complexity pay?".
5. **The risk-model metrics**: the realized/predicted variance ratio and the explanatory-power ratio. They are correctly defined, cheap, and they caught a real 2.7× risk underestimation. Keep them; add a null distribution (VaR-exception or DM test) rather than a band.
6. **Point-in-time crisis labelling.** `compute_crisis_threshold` uses only data ≤ training end. This is a verified no-look-ahead guarantee worth preserving verbatim.
7. **The scenario matrix as a written specification.** Scenarios A–E, with a 1/N null and a PCA ablation, are the intellectual core of the unit. They need to be made reachable and significance-aware, not re-invented.
8. **Metric arithmetic that is simply correct**: log-space MDD converted to percentage drawdown, geometric annualisation of log returns, standard Gini, Meucci ENB from factor risk contributions. These should not be touched in a redesign.
9. **The probabilistic-Sharpe implementation in preference to the doc's haircut formula.** The code is better than the document here; keep the code.

## Verdict

The statistical *primitives* of U14 are in good shape: exact Wilcoxon, correct Holm stepdown, robust per-fold aggregation, correct drawdown and annualisation arithmetic, a well-conceived benchmark ladder, a correctly specified variance-ratio test, and a point-in-time crisis definition. The unit's *assembly* is not. The decision layer never reads a p-value, computes no adjusted p-value, cannot test the one metric that measures the strategy's objective, silently drops the ninth fold, folds the holdout into the test family, treats "no data" as "PCA ties", cannot emit scenarios D or E, and selects E\* with a composite whose entropy and drawdown terms are hard-coded zeros — so the run that produced the only real-data report (`results/diagnostic`) simultaneously recommends "Adopt PCA" on zero tests and flags a critical 2.7× risk underestimation. The unit is therefore doing careful measurement and then discarding it at the decision boundary. The redesign should keep the measurement layer almost intact and delete or merge the three overlapping decision surfaces into one significance-aware evaluator with an explicit inconclusive state and a separate holdout.

Score: 4/10
