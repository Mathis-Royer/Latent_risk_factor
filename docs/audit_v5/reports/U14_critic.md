## Five questions

**1. Purpose and original intent.** The unit exists to make the VAE's complexity *falsifiable*. `docs/Benchmark_latent_risk_factor.md:5`: "la complexité du VAE n'est justifiée que si elle produit un bénéfice OOS mesurable et statistiquement significatif par rapport à des alternatives plus simples". The machinery is specified as: three metric layers with H_OOS as the primary endpoint (`:166-175`), per-fold paired differences on a composite score (`:211-213`), Wilcoxon + bootstrap CI (`:215-216`), Holm-Bonferroni over "6 benchmarks × 4 métriques primaires = 24 tests" (`:224`), crisis/calm split (`:228`), and a five-way decision matrix (`:287-311`). `docs/diagnostic.md` adds a second, independent apparatus: ten 0-100 composite scores, five scenarios, and a rule engine with causal graphs. The intent is sound and unusually well specified. Execution diverges from intent on nearly every axis.

**2. State of the art for this objective.** A strategy-vs-benchmark-family comparison in 2026 would (a) pre-register **one** primary endpoint and one comparison, with the rest explicitly secondary (`Harvey, Liu & Zhu 2016` argue a t > 3 hurdle for that reason); (b) compare against the *best* of the alternatives using a dependence-aware data-snooping test — White's Reality Check (2000), Hansen's SPA (2005), or Romano-Wolf stepwise — rather than Holm over a 24-cell grid; (c) infer on **daily paired returns** with a stationary/block bootstrap (Politis-Romano 1994), not on 9 fold-level scalars; (d) deflate honestly with the Deflated Sharpe Ratio (Bailey & López de Prado 2014) and compute PBO by actual CSCV over the *configurations actually tried* (Bailey, Borwein, López de Prado & Zhu 2017); (e) state a power calculation *before* running, so a null is interpretable; (f) evaluate performance net of the costs the strategy actually pays, and against a passive index. Gilboa & Schmeidler and DeMiguel et al. (2009) already set the null the unit must beat.

**3. What prevents it, evidenced.** (i) The assessment scale: 9 folds in `results/report.json:287`, 1 fold in `results/diagnostic/report.json` — against ~34 specified (`docs/...Benchmark...:59`), and against 24 tests. That combination makes Holm-rejection arithmetically impossible (CRI-04). (ii) The primary endpoint does not exist for any benchmark (`src/benchmarks/base.py:199-210`; absent from all `results/*_fold_metrics.csv`), so the family silently shrinks to 18 tests of secondary metrics (`results/report.json:263`). (iii) The decision does not read the tests at all (`src/walk_forward/selection.py:117-124`; `p_threshold` declared at `:79` and never used). (iv) Regime robustness is hardwired off (`src/integration/pipeline.py:790`, `:1193`). (v) The composite-score system has no performance term (`src/integration/composite_scores.py:1561-1572`) and the Phase A composite runs on placeholders (`pipeline.py:1494-1496`).

**4. Real vs self-imposed constraints.**
- *Real*: fat tails and volatility clustering ⇒ distribution-free inference is appropriate; a benchmark family of six ⇒ some multiplicity control is required; only ~30 years of daily history ⇒ a limited number of non-overlapping six-month blocks.
- *Self-imposed*: fold-level inference instead of daily block bootstrap (the data support the latter; this is the single largest source of lost power); a 4-metric × 6-benchmark grid instead of one endpoint; 9 folds instead of 34 (a compute choice, not a data limit); treating entropy as "not computable" for benchmarks when `pca_factor_rp` already computes it internally; Holm instead of SPA; letter-graded scenarios; ten 0-100 scores with ~20 hand-set thresholds; selecting hyperparameters on the same OOS folds used for the verdict; averaging Sharpe across folds instead of pooling.

**5. What should be removed, merged, moved.**
- **Remove** the composite 0-100 score system (`composite_scores.py`, ~1500 lines of thresholds with no PnL input). It is uncalibrated, cannot detect a losing strategy, and gives a second, contradictory verdict.
- **Remove** the A-E scenario enum from code. Replace with a three-state gate on one endpoint: `beats_best_passive / indistinguishable / loses`, plus an explicit `insufficient_evidence` when folds < 24. As written, the enum has two unreachable members (D, E) and one inverted (C).
- **Remove** `wins/total` counting (`selection.py:124`) and Sharpe from the decision family.
- **Merge** `selection.check_deployment_criteria` and `diagnostics.benchmark_comparison` into one comparison kernel — they already disagree in metric set and aggregation.
- **Merge** the three entropy normalizations into one metric registry; delete the K-normalized "legacy" variant (`metrics.py:684`).
- **Move** crisis/regime decomposition from an optional `None` into the core test — or delete the anti-cyclical claim from the thesis.
- **Move** the holdout evaluation (doc Étape 6) into the report, and **move** HP selection off the OOS folds.
- **Delete** the ERC row whenever it falls back to 1/N; better, make the fallback a hard failure.

## Root-cause hypotheses

**RC-1 — The decision layer was built as a reporting layer and never reconnected.** Symptoms: dead `p_threshold` (CRI-01), scenario C from zero wins (CRI-02), unreachable D/E, a second comparison engine that nothing reads (CRI-09), composite scores with no PnL (MAJ-03). The code computes a verdict-shaped object (`scenario`, `recommendation`) from the *cheapest available* signal (medians, counts) because the expensive signal (tests) lived in a different module with a different schema. Structural alternatives: (a) one decision kernel — a pure function of a pre-registered test record, from which every human-facing artifact is generated; (b) delete the scenario enum in favour of a three-state gate plus `insufficient_evidence`; (c) make the verdict an effect size with a CI and forbid categorical output entirely until power thresholds are met.

**RC-2 — No single primary endpoint, so the family grew until it had no power.** Symptoms: 18 vs 24 tests (CRI-03), p-floor above the corrected threshold (CRI-04), Sharpe promoted and crisis return dropped (MAJ-02), `total` denominators (MAJ-12), ERC duplicate inflating `m` (MAJ-08). Multiplicity was treated as a formatting problem (Holm over whatever rows happened to exist) rather than a design problem. Structural alternatives: (a) one pre-registered endpoint tested once against the best alternative, everything else descriptive; (b) a single composite endpoint exactly as the doc specifies (`H·_OOS − λ·max(0, MDD−θ)`) tested once; (c) replace Holm with Hansen's SPA / White's Reality Check over the six benchmark series so the dependence structure buys back power instead of being ignored.

**RC-3 — Every construct has several simultaneous definitions.** Symptoms: four entropy normalizations and two artifacts that disagree by 2× (MAJ-11), four EP thresholds (MAJ-04), two Sharpe formulas (MAJ-02), two comparison engines (CRI-09), four different report formats (`report.txt`, `report.json`, `diagnostic_report.md`, `strategy_comparison.csv`) that are not derivations of one another. Structural alternatives: (a) a metric registry — one function, one sign, one unit, one normalization per named metric, consumed by VAE, benchmarks, diagnostics and report; (b) version the schema and make additions explicit rather than parallel; (c) delete every metric that is not in the registry, including the legacy K-normalized entropy.

**RC-4 — Evaluation scale is decoupled from the inference design.** The protocol specifies 34 folds; runs deliver 9 and 1; the machinery still assumes a distribution of fold statistics. Symptoms: CRI-04, MAJ-09, the "too few paired observations" skip that still emits a decision. Structural alternatives: (a) hard precondition — no verdict below 24 folds, only `insufficient_evidence`; (b) move inference to daily paired return differences with a stationary bootstrap (orders of magnitude more power, and it directly measures what the strategy is claimed to improve); (c) pre-register a power calculation and derive the minimum fold count from the target effect size.

**RC-5 — Self-assessment is used as a substitute for external validation.** Symptoms: composite scores with no performance term (MAJ-03), health checks that pass any positive Sharpe (CRI-09), placeholder Phase A inputs (CRI-07), asserted rule confidences (MIN-07), "OK" for a strategy that loses to 1/N and to the index. The system grades its own machinery and calls the grade "health". Structural alternatives: (a) remove the composite score system entirely and gate only on an external, benchmark-relative criterion; (b) keep scores strictly as engineering telemetry, with the words "health"/"grade"/"A-F" removed so they cannot be mistaken for a performance verdict; (c) require every component to be expressed relative to a reference model (var_ratio vs Ledoit-Wolf, not vs 1.0).

**RC-6 — Silent degradation is the house style, and it is invisible to the tests.** Symptoms: ERC → 1/N (`erc.py:298-305`), NaN → 0 returns (`metrics.py:629`), `EP = 0.5` placeholder, `H_oos = 0.0`, missing metrics dropped with `continue` (`reporting.py:66-70`), `p_value = 1.0` for degenerate tests. Each transforms a failure into a plausible number that then flows into the family. Structural alternatives: (a) fail-closed with an explicit `status` field on every metric and benchmark row, and recompute `m` from the surviving rows; (b) attach provenance and require a minimum data coverage per metric before it can enter a test; (c) report missingness as a first-class metric in every table.

## Verdict

The measurement layer here is competent; the decision layer is not merely weak but structurally incapable of doing its job, and in the one real-data run it reached the wrong conclusion. The strategy's own objective (factor entropy) is never compared against a benchmark, so the central claim of the project is untested; the one metric the spec designates as the crisis test is computed for the VAE and then discarded; the multiplicity correction cannot reject at any effect size given 9 folds; the p-value labelled "corrected" is the raw one; the deployment rule ignores significance entirely because the parameter that would carry it is declared and never read; and the scenario logic converts 0/4 wins into "PCA ≈ VAE, adopt PCA" while leaving the two scenarios that describe defeat unreachable. Two evaluation engines coexist and contradict each other in the same folder — one says the risk model is CRITICALLY miscalibrated, the other says adopt PCA for cost reasons — and the 0-100 composite score that bridges them contains no return, volatility or drawdown term, so it would certify a strategy with a negative Sharpe as healthy.

The steelman is worth stating because it is the strongest thing in the unit: the VAE-vs-PCA comparison does show a real-looking Sharpe advantage (median Δ = +0.361, CI [+0.305, +0.474], raw p = 0.0078), a lower drawdown than PCA (Δ = −0.0091, CI excludes 0), and lower volatility than PCA+vol. On a pre-registered single-endpoint test at α = 0.05 that result would be reportable. But it is not reportable here, for four independent reasons: (i) Sharpe is classified as a *diagnostic* by the project's own spec, so winning on it while losing on entropy and crisis return is not the pre-declared success criterion; (ii) the folds that produced it were also used for hyperparameter selection, so the intervals are not OOS with respect to the pipeline; (iii) the underlying run used E* = 1 epoch — a smoke test, not a strategy; and (iv) with the family as configured, no outcome could ever have been labelled significant, so the "null result" the project is now reasoning from is not a measurement. The unit is therefore simultaneously over-corrected (killing its own best evidence via an ill-posed 24-cell family) and under-corrected (a selection-contaminated OOS, a duplicate benchmark, a gain metric at 1 epoch). The fix is not a patch: it is to replace the letter-graded, median-counted, 18-test apparatus with one pre-registered endpoint, daily block-bootstrap inference, a fail-closed benchmark family, and a holdout. Until that exists, every conclusion in `results/` — including "Scenario C" — is an artifact of the decision machinery rather than a fact about the strategy.

Score: 2/10
