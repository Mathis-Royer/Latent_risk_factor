## Five questions

1. **Purpose and original intent (per spec).** DVT §4.5 defines: uniform sampling across all history, current relevance deferred to inference (`DVT…:596`); Adam with default moments (`:602`); η₀ = 10⁻⁴ with ReduceLROnPlateau(0.5, patience 5) (`:606-610`); 100-epoch ceiling (`:614`); early stopping on validation ELBO, patience 10, best-weight restore, E\* transferred to deployment (`:616-625`); quarterly full retraining (`:630-640`). The one-sentence intent: produce a *pure risk model* trained on every regime, with convergence controlled by a validation ELBO rather than by a fixed budget.

2. **State of the art for this objective.** A 10 M-parameter CNN trained on ~24 years of 739 stocks should use: a pre-committed compute budget with a smooth deterministic LR schedule (one-cycle / cosine; Loshchilov & Hutter 2017); AdamW with decay excluded from biases and scalars (Loshchilov & Hutter 2019); deterministic deployment via an EMA/SWA average or seed ensemble rather than "train exactly E\* epochs" (Izmailov et al. 2018); capacity set from *independent* observations and explicit regularisation; if a co-movement prior is wanted, β-annealing or free-bits over a fixed schedule (Bowman et al. 2016; Kingma et al. 2016) with an ablation; and early stopping only on purged future data (López de Prado 2018). None of these requires a curriculum whose clock is a stopping-time variable.

3. **What prevents it (evidenced, not assumed).** (i) λ_co, β, phase boundaries and the LR horizon are fractions of `max_epochs` while termination is convergence-based → Phase 3 and random batching are unreachable (CRI-01). (ii) Deployment sets `max_epochs=E*`, re-basing every schedule → Phase B is not "the Phase A model with more data" (CRI-02). (iii) "No validation in deployment" does not exist in code (CRI-03). (iv) E\* is a noisy argmin on a nearly flat curve (CRI-04). (v) Every training hyperparameter differs from the spec (CRI-05). (vi) Capacity is licensed by overlapping-window counts (CRI-08). (vii) No ablation and no test asserts any curriculum benefit (CRI-11). (viii) The one real run's diagnostics report training as "unavailable" (CRI-18).

4. **Real vs self-imposed constraints.** *Real*: one 16 GB machine without CUDA, 739 usable stocks × ~24 years, one fit per fold; the information budget (~17.7 k stock-years, ~400 independent 21-day blocks) is a hard fact. *Self-imposed*: 500 epochs when the val curve flattens by epoch ~170; a 10 M-parameter model whose size was licensed by a stride-1 window count; an 18-config × n-starts HP grid; the three-phase λ_co curriculum; k-means stratification; two different patience clocks; a second full training run per fold for a 2-year data increment.

5. **What should be removed, merged or moved.**
   - **Remove** the three-phase λ_co curriculum. Its final phase is unreachable, its best epoch sits at the Phase 1/2 boundary, and it has never been ablated.
   - **Remove** ReduceLROnPlateau; it fired once, after the best epoch. Move LR control into a fixed schedule inside a committed budget.
   - **Remove** the E\* transfer and the separate Phase B optimisation; **merge** Phase A and Phase B into one fit per fold on all data ≤ t_k with a declared checkpoint rule (or an EMA average).
   - **Remove** k-means stratification in its current form (computed once, unstandardised, from the validation period).
   - **Move** model selection off the epoch axis; **move** the capacity guard from window counts to independent-observation counts.
   - **Move** the training history into the diagnostic payload that already exists.

## Root-cause hypotheses

**RC-1 — Schedule variables are tied to a budget while stopping is tied to convergence (CRI-01, -02, -04, -06).** λ_co, β, phase boundaries and the LR horizon are all fractions of `max_epochs`, but the run terminates wherever the ELBO argmin happens to be. Two clocks disagree: a curriculum that assumes it will traverse 100 % of a schedule, and a stopper that routinely stops at 30–45 %. Every artefact that reads "E\*" then pretends the clocks coincide. One design error produces four symptoms: Phase 3 never runs, random batching never runs, the LR scheduler never usefully fires, and Phase B's curriculum differs from Phase A's.
*Alternatives:* (a) **Fixed-budget training** — commit to N epochs, one-cycle LR, select the checkpoint by validation, deploy by re-running the same N-epoch schedule with an EMA average; trade-off: no early exit, ~30 % wasted compute, full reproducibility. (b) **Stopping-time curriculum** — make λ_co and β functions of validation progress (e.g. λ_co ∝ max(0, 1 − k·epochs-since-best)) so the schedule cannot outlive the run; trade-off: adaptive schedules are harder to reproduce across folds. (c) **No schedule** — constant λ_co, fixed LR, fixed budget; trade-off: forfeits a possibly real but unmeasured benefit.

**RC-2 — "Deployment has no validation" is a narrative, not an implementation (CRI-03, -04, -12, -18).** The design wants a deterministic production model, but code keeps a selection slice in every path — a temporal 2-year slice in Phase A, a positional 10 %-stocks slice in Phase B — and restores best weights from it. Because the slice identity is an artefact of column ordering, the selection criterion is not a declared object. "No validation", "deterministic", "E\* epochs" describe an intention the code does not implement.
*Alternatives:* (a) **One validation contract** (future 24 months, purged) used identically in both phases and recorded; trade-off: those 24 months are unavailable for training. (b) **Deploy an average, not a checkpoint** — average the last k epochs or three seeds; trade-off: more compute, but removes epoch selection as a degree of freedom. (c) **Deploy on all data with a fixed budget and no selection**, controlling overfit by KL/dropout/decay; trade-off: no empirical guard at all, which is only defensible if an overfitting study exists — it does not.

**RC-3 — The data budget is counted in overlapping windows, not in information (CRI-07, -08, -17).** Counting sliding windows as samples inflates N by 21–500×, licensing a 10 M-parameter CNN; that CNN demands a batching scheme that reduces per-batch independence further; and AU is then measured on one synchronous block while the monitoring band is calibrated on another sample. Capacity, training regime and monitoring statistic are all calibrated against the same fiction.
*Alternatives:* (a) **Size on independent observations** (stock-years or non-overlapping windows), which forces K≈5–15 and a far smaller CNN; trade-off: gives up capacity the VAE may need for nonlinearity. (b) **Keep capacity but impose the regularisation the honest ratio implies** (heavy decay, strong dropout, seed ensembling) and report effective r; trade-off: more hyperparameters and compute. (c) **Change the unit of learning** — train on non-overlapping or monthly-strided windows with larger batches, treating market days rather than windows as the sample; trade-off: fewer gradient steps, longer wall-clock per useful update.

**RC-4 — Documented hyperparameters are aspirations; the operating point is set elsewhere (CRI-05, -13, -15, -16).** The spec fixes Adam/10⁻⁴/100/patience 10; `config.py` ships 250/5×10⁻³/20; the notebook ships 500/5×10⁻³/20/LR-patience 30; the only real run ships 500/5×10⁻³/50/wd 10⁻³. The mechanism meant to resolve this — Phase A HP selection — is a stub: `_evaluate_hp_config` returns `"H_oos": 0.0`, `"mdd_oos": 0.0`, `"explanatory_power": 0.5` placeholders, so every config passes the EP ≥ 0.40 gate, `H_norm` is identically 0, and the score reduces to the estimation penalty; ties keep the first entry. The learning rate is therefore never actually selected.
*Alternatives:* (a) **One source of truth** — a resolved config serialised into every artefact, with docs quoting it rather than the reverse; trade-off: documentation must be regenerated and historical claims become explicitly stale. (b) **A real criterion** — rank configs on the out-of-sample fold score the DVT already claims to use, and refuse to run Phase A until `H_oos` is computed; trade-off: a full downstream pipeline per config. (c) **Freeze the training regime** — one documented configuration, no search over training parameters, sensitivity reported as a separate study; trade-off: no per-fold adaptation.

**RC-5 — Training has never run at the scale the theory requires, and the artefacts cosmetically hide it (CRI-11, -18).** The only real-data run is one fit (E\*=164 of 500), one 6.3-year OOS, no Phase A/B pair, no retraining, no ablation; its diagnostic reports training as unavailable, and the V1/V2 numbers exist only in prose. Every load-bearing claim in §4.5 is therefore `doc`-only.
*Alternatives:* (a) **One honest scaling experiment** — 3 seeds × 2 epoch budgets × (curriculum on/off), reporting val and OOS ELBO plus fold Sharpe; trade-off: days of compute, but it is the minimum required to keep the curriculum. (b) **Delete the curriculum and the two-phase protocol**, train one model per fold with a fixed budget, and stop claiming unobserved behaviour; trade-off: less theoretical elegance, far fewer untested invariants. (c) **Persist what already exists** — write `fit_result` into the diagnostic payload so the 215-epoch curve becomes auditable; trade-off: none, and it exposes rather than repairs RC-1/RC-2.

## Verdict

The training loop is competently *engineered* — σ² clamping after each step, AMP with unscale-before-clip, gradient accumulation with a flushed tail, best-state restore with key assertions, NaN-loss step skipping, a single GPU→CPU sync per epoch. It is also, scientifically, the weakest link in the strategy's chain of claims. The single retained real-data run shows the curriculum terminating inside Phase 2 with λ_co = 0.29, so the "temporary scaffolding" was never removed, random batching never executed, and Phase 3 — the phase the theory credits with discovering non-linear structure — was never reached; the validation curve began degrading exactly where annealing began, and the best epoch is flat to within 0.1 % over eight epochs. The deployment stage then contradicts its own specification twice over: it re-bases the entire schedule onto E\*, so it does not reproduce the model it claims to transfer, and it silently holds out the last 10 % of stocks by column order to select among E\* epochs — while the documents assert that no validation set exists. Every nominal hyperparameter (lr ×50, epochs ×5, weight decay ×100, patience ×5, Adam→AdamW, undocumented gradient clipping) has drifted away from the spec with no recorded decision, and the phase that was supposed to select them assigns placeholder scores to the two terms that carry the signal. Underneath all of it sits the deepest problem: the model's capacity was licensed by counting overlapping sliding windows as independent observations, so the 10 M-parameter CNN is roughly two orders of magnitude larger than its own guard intends, and the training regime, the batching scheme and the AU statistic are all calibrated against that fiction. None of this is a bug to patch; it is a training architecture whose clocks, selection criteria and data budget are mutually inconsistent, and the fix is to collapse the two-phase protocol into one honest fit with a committed budget, a declared validation contract, an averaged deployment model, and no curriculum that its own stopping rule can outrun.

Score: 3/10
